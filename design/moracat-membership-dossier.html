Moracat · UX & Customer Experience Strategy

The Membership
Dossier

The trusted identity every cat owner wants to carry.

Blueprint · Pre-Design 11 Sections 120 UX Rules Experience-first Bilingual · AR/EN

This document is a design brief for the people who will build Moracat — human or AI. It defines the feeling, the psychology, the sequence, and the standards of the experience, and leaves every pixel, component, and line of code to the team that implements it. It contains no wireframes, no screens, no code. It answers a harder question first: what should it feel like to belong to Moracat, and why would someone stay for years?

00 · Orientation

The one sentence this whole document defends

Moracat is not a discount app with a cat theme. It is a membership identity for the modern cat owner — where the savings are proof of value, and belonging is the product.

Read that carefully, because it is the fork in the road that decides whether Moracat becomes a beloved institution or a coupon wallet people cancel in month three. Every recommendation in this handbook flows from it. When a future decision is ambiguous, the tie-breaker is always: does this make a person feel like a recognised, cared-for member — or like someone hunting for a code?

Who this is for, and how to use it

Written for the product, design, and engineering team (and any AI agent) that will build the Moracat web and mobile experience. You have complete freedom over visual design, components, stack, and architecture. What you do not have freedom over is the intent: the emotional outcomes, the trust sequence, the friction budget, and the rules in Section 09. Treat those as constraints. Treat everything else as invitation.

How to read the callouts

Principle blocks (green) are non-negotiable design intents. Challenge blocks (dashed red) are places where I push back on the brief or a common assumption — read these closely; they are where the real strategy lives. Beyond the brief blocks (gold) are opportunities the original request didn't ask for but that would meaningfully improve the experience.

Three assumptions I am challenging up front

Challenge 1 · "Discounts are the value."

Discounts are the easiest thing to copy and the fastest way to feel cheap. A store can hand out a free loyalty card tomorrow. If Moracat's headline is "save money," it will be compared on price and lose. The durable value is portable identity + curated trust + effortless convenience across the whole cat-owner life. Savings should feel like a member perk, never the pitch.

Challenge 2 · "The Cat ID is a nice card."

A beautiful card that does nothing sits unused, and unused things get cancelled. The Cat ID must be given a job people actually need: recovering a lost cat, checking in at the vet in one tap, redeeming instantly at a partner, and holding the cat's health record. Utility is what turns a card into a habit, and habit is what turns a subscriber into a member for life. Section 05 rebuilds the Cat ID around this.

Challenge 3 · "Members will feel the value."

People cancel what they cannot feel. A subscription's value is invisible by default — the money leaves quietly every month while the benefits stay abstract. Moracat must make value legible: surfaced savings totals, benefits used, moments of care remembered. If a member can't answer "what did this give me lately?" in two seconds, renewal is already at risk.

With that framing set, the rest of the handbook builds the experience — philosophy first, then the journey, then the machinery of trust, psychology, rules, and priorities.

01

Part I · The Feeling

UX Philosophy

Before a single feature is designed, agree on the emotional and ethical contract Moracat makes with a person. Features change; this contract shouldn't. It is the spine every future decision hangs from.

The experience Moracat should deliver, in one line: the quiet confidence of belonging to something that takes your cat as seriously as you do.

Cat owners are not casual about their animals — they are devoted, often quietly, sometimes fiercely. The category they live in (pet stores, clinics, random online tips) treats them transactionally. Moracat's opportunity is to be the one relationship that feels the opposite: recognising, competent, warm, and unhurried. Not loud. Not gamified into exhaustion. Membership should feel like being known.

The emotions to design for

Design is emotion management. Every screen either adds or subtracts from how a person feels. Moracat should reliably produce six feelings, in this order of importance:

Belonging
"I'm part of this." The core emotion. Membership, identity, recognition. A person should feel inside a circle, not outside a paywall. Everything else is downstream of belonging.
Trust / safety
"These people are careful, and my cat and my data are safe with them." The permission slip for everything — payment, health records, referrals to real clinics.
Pride
"My cat has an identity." A gentle, shareable pride. The kind that makes someone show the Cat ID to a friend without being asked.
Ease / relief
"That was effortless." The feeling of a burden lifted — finding a good vet, redeeming a benefit, remembering a vaccination date without trying.
Delight
"Oh — that's lovely." Small, unexpected warmth. Used sparingly so it stays real. Delight is the seasoning, not the meal.
Smart value
"This is clearly worth it." Not "I got a bargain" but "I'm a savvy owner who made a good decision." Value as self-image, not price.
Emotion before feature

When two designs are equally functional, choose the one that produces belonging and trust first, ease second, delight third. Never trade trust for delight, and never trade ease for cleverness.

Brand personality

If Moracat were a person at the door of a members' club, they would be the calm, impeccably competent host who remembers your cat's name, never rushes you, and quietly handles the hard things. Concretely, the personality is:

Is

Warm & composed

Kind without being saccharine. Confident without being cold. Speaks plainly, like a trusted friend who happens to be an expert.

Is

Quietly premium

Premium through restraint and craft, not gold gradients and the word "luxury." The confidence to leave white space and say less.

Is not

Not a coupon barker

Never shouty, never "LIMITED TIME!!", never manipulative countdowns. Urgency is earned by real events, not manufactured.

Is not

Not a cutesy toy

Cats invite kitsch; resist it. Affectionate, yes; infantilising, no. The owner is an adult making a considered choice.

The best membership feels less like access and more like recognition.

What users expect (and what will surprise them)

People arrive with a mental model borrowed from other subscriptions — some helpful, some to be gently corrected.

They expect

Instant access after paying. A card or number they can actually use. Clear, honest pricing with no surprise renewals. Real, redeemable benefits — not "up to 50%*" fine print. To be treated like a paying customer, not a lead. Fast, human help when something goes wrong.

They will be surprised (in a good way) by

How fast the Cat ID appears and how good it feels to have one. How the membership remembers their cat and speaks to it by name. How genuinely useful the ID is beyond discounts. How the brand proactively cares (reminders, check-ins) instead of only extracting. How easy it is to leave — which, paradoxically, is why they'll stay.

The experience principles

Ten principles that govern how every interaction should behave. Treat these as the constitution.

01 · Recognition first
Greet the member and their cat by name. The product should feel like it knows who it's talking to from the second interaction onward.
02 · Effort is the enemy
Ruthlessly minimise steps, fields, taps, and decisions. Every unit of effort is a small tax on belonging.
03 · Value stays visible
Never let the benefits go abstract. Savings, perks, and care are surfaced, tallied, and remembered.
04 · Trust precedes ask
Earn confidence before requesting money, data, or commitment — in that sequence, every time.
05 · One clear action
Each moment has a single obvious next step. Secondary options recede; they never compete.
06 · Honest by default
No dark patterns, no hidden terms, no fake scarcity. If a partner isn't in their city yet, say so.
07 · Calm over clever
Prefer the quiet, obvious solution to the impressive one. Delight is rationed so it stays meaningful.
08 · Care, don't extract
The relationship gives before it takes — reminders, tips, warmth — so asks feel fair.
09 · The cat is the hero
The emotional centre is always the animal, never the app. Design in service of the bond between owner and cat.
10 · Leaving is easy
Cancelling, pausing, and exporting are simple and dignified. Freedom is what makes membership feel safe to enter.

Trust principles

Trust in this category is fragile and specific. It is not one thing — it is four promises kept simultaneously.

Promise

Financial honesty

You will always know exactly what you pay, when you'll be charged again, and how to stop. No renewal ambushes, ever.

Promise

Data stewardship

What we collect, why, and who sees it is plain and minimal. The cat's health data especially is treated as sacred.

Promise

Partner integrity

Every recommended vet and store is vetted. A member's trust in Moracat is only as strong as its weakest partner.

Promise

Competent care

When something breaks — a failed redemption, a billing error — it's fixed fast, by someone who acts like they care.

Assumption to retire · "Premium means adding more."

Premium is a subtraction discipline, not an addition one. The instinct will be to pack the experience with tiers, badges, points, streaks, and features to "justify the price." Resist it. The most premium experiences feel empty in the best way — spacious, certain, uncluttered. Complexity is the tell of a cheap product trying to look expensive.

Simplicity principles

Simplicity is not the absence of features — it's the absence of friction and doubt. Enforce it with these rules:

Default, don't ask
Pre-fill, pre-select, and infer wherever possible. Every question the product answers itself is a question the member doesn't have to.
Progressive depth
Show the simple path to everyone; reveal advanced options only to those who reach for them. Never front-load complexity.
One idea per moment
Each step teaches or asks for exactly one thing. If a moment needs a paragraph to explain, it's doing too much.
Recognise over recall
Never make people remember something the product could show them. The Cat ID number, the renewal date, the benefits — always in reach.
Make the right thing easy
The healthiest, safest, most valuable action should also be the most obvious and lowest-effort one.
02

Part I · The Arc

The Member Journey

The journey mapped as experiences and emotions, not screens. Each stage names what the person is trying to do, how they feel, what they fear, what success looks like, and the two levers that matter most everywhere: a chance to build trust and a chance to delight. The build team decides the screens; this defines what those screens must accomplish emotionally.

A membership isn't bought once — it's re-bought every month in the member's mind. The journey below is a loop, not a line: the goal of every stage is to earn the next renewal without ever mentioning renewal.

01

First Impression

Awareness →
Curiosity

◎ Goal

Understand in seconds what Moracat is and whether it's "for someone like me and my cat."

♥ Emotion

Mild curiosity mixed with reflexive skepticism — "is this just another discount thing?"

△ Concerns

Feels gimmicky; unclear what I actually get; suspicion of a catch or hidden cost.

✓ Desired outcome

A clear, confident "I get it — this is a membership for cat owners" and enough intrigue to look closer.

⛨ Build trust

Lead with identity and care, not price. Show real partner names/logos and a plain one-line promise. Credibility signals visible immediately.

✦ Delight

Speak to the cat, not just the owner ("Give your cat a name the world will recognise"). Warmth in the very first words.

✕ Mistakes to avoid

Leading with "SAVE 50%." Vague hero copy that could describe any app. A wall of features before the person understands the one idea. Fake urgency on first contact.

02

Consideration

Evaluating →
Deciding

◎ Goal

Answer "is this worth it for me?" — weigh cost against benefits I'll actually use.

♥ Emotion

Calculating and slightly guarded. Wants to feel smart, fears being upsold or locked in.

△ Concerns

Will I use it enough? Are the partners near me? Can I cancel? Is my data/payment safe? What's the real total?

✓ Desired outcome

Confident "this pays for itself and I can leave anytime" — enough certainty to commit without anxiety.

⛨ Build trust

Transparent pricing with the renewal terms shown before signup. "Cancel anytime" stated plainly. A break-even framing ("most members cover the fee in one vet visit"). Partner coverage shown honestly by city.

✦ Delight

Let them preview what their Cat ID could look like with their cat's name before paying — a taste of belonging that makes the decision emotional, not just rational.

✕ Mistakes to avoid

Hiding the renewal price or auto-renew terms. Overwhelming with tiers. Implying nationwide coverage that doesn't exist. Making "cancel" feel hard or unmentioned. Pressuring with countdown timers.

03

The Commitment

Signing up →
Paying

◎ Goal

Join and pay with as little effort and doubt as possible, then get in.

♥ Emotion

A small leap of faith. Hopeful but alert — the payment moment is peak vulnerability.

△ Concerns

Is my card safe? Am I about to be trapped in auto-renew? Did I just make a mistake? Too many fields = second thoughts.

✓ Desired outcome

"That was quick and safe" — payment done in a few taps with total clarity on what happens next.

⛨ Build trust

Show the exact charge, the exact next-charge date, and the cancel path on the payment moment itself. Offer trusted local payment (mada, Apple Pay, STC Pay). Security cues that are real, not decorative padlock theatre.

✦ Delight

The instant after payment is the single most important delight moment in the entire product — see Stage 4. Make the confirmation feel like a welcome, not a receipt.

✕ Mistakes to avoid

Long forms. Asking for anything not strictly needed to charge and greet them. Surprise fees at the last step. A cold "Payment successful" with no emotional payoff. Pre-ticked add-ons.

04

Welcome & First Value

Activation →
"I'm in"

◎ Goal

Receive the thing I paid for — my cat's identity — and feel it was worth it, immediately.

♥ Emotion

Anticipation flipping to pride and relief. This is the emotional peak; get it right and retention starts here.

△ Concerns

"Now what?" Confusion about how to actually use the benefits. Fear the value was hype.

✓ Desired outcome

The Cat ID exists, feels special, and the member knows the one thing to do next. First benefit understood within minutes.

⛨ Build trust

Deliver exactly what was promised, instantly — the Cat ID Number and digital card, ready to use. A confirmation of the terms they agreed to, in writing, warmly worded.

✦ Delight

Reveal the Cat ID as a small ceremony — the cat's name, a unique number, a moment of "this is real." Invite adding to the phone wallet and sharing. This is Moracat's "Spotify Wrapped" moment: engineer it deliberately.

✕ Mistakes to avoid

Dumping the member into a generic dashboard. A boring, instant, ceremony-free ID. Ten setup tasks before any payoff. Hiding the ID behind more forms. Wasting the highest-emotion moment on admin.

05

Everyday Membership

Habit →
Belonging

◎ Goal

Get ongoing value between big moments — feel the membership is "on" even when I'm not buying anything.

♥ Emotion

Quiet reassurance. Ideally a low hum of "I'm taken care of," punctuated by occasional warmth.

△ Concerns

"Am I actually using this?" The silent-value problem: money leaves monthly, benefit feels abstract. This is where churn is seeded.

✓ Desired outcome

The member feels the membership is quietly working — reminders, care, and easy access to benefits keep it present without nagging.

⛨ Build trust

Proactive, useful care that expects nothing back: a vaccination reminder, a seasonal tip, a check-in. Give before you take. Keep the running savings/benefits tally visible so value never goes silent.

✦ Delight

Remember the cat's birthday. A warm note on a member anniversary. An occasional, genuinely useful surprise perk. Presence without pressure.

✕ Mistakes to avoid

Going silent for weeks (out of sight = cancelled). Over-notifying (nagging = muted, then cancelled). Only ever contacting them to sell or renew. Letting the value become invisible.

06

Redemption in the Wild

The truth →
moment

◎ Goal

Actually use a benefit — a discount at a clinic or store — smoothly, in front of a cashier or vet, without awkwardness.

♥ Emotion

Slight social risk ("will this work, or will I look foolish?") resolving into satisfaction when it does.

△ Concerns

Will the partner honour it? Will I fumble at the counter? Is my card/ID ready offline if there's no signal?

✓ Desired outcome

Redemption is instant and dignified — a glance, a tap, done. The member feels the savings were real and effortless.

⛨ Build trust

This is the moment that proves the whole product. It must work every time. Make the ID accessible offline. Ensure partners are briefed so staff recognise it instantly. Confirm the saving afterward so the value is banked in memory.

✦ Delight

A gentle "you just saved SAR 45 — that's SAR 320 this year" right after redemption. The saving, made vivid at the exact moment of relief, is worth ten marketing emails.

✕ Mistakes to avoid

A redemption that fails or confuses partner staff — this is catastrophic and churns members instantly. Requiring signal at the counter. Making the member explain what Moracat is to a blank-faced cashier. Never confirming the saving.

07

The Renewal Moment

Recommitting →
Staying

◎ Goal

Decide (consciously or not) whether the last period was worth it and the next one will be too.

♥ Emotion

Quiet evaluation, sometimes anxiety about "am I wasting money?" A charge notice can trigger a cancel reflex.

△ Concerns

"Did I get my money's worth?" If the answer isn't obvious and positive, the default drifts toward cancelling.

✓ Desired outcome

Renewal feels obvious and earned — the member sees clear proof of value and re-commits with confidence, or renews without even thinking about it.

⛨ Build trust

Notify before charging, never after ("you'll renew in 3 days — here's the year you had"). Show a value recap: saved, benefits used, care received. Radical honesty at renewal builds a member who trusts you for years and tells others.

✦ Delight

A personalised year-in-review of their cat's membership — savings, milestones, "Muezza's first year as a member." Turn a billing event into a moment of pride.

✕ Mistakes to avoid

Silent auto-charges that feel sneaky (the #1 trust-killer and refund-driver, especially in KSA). Making cancellation hard or hidden — it backfires into resentment and chargebacks. Renewing without ever reminding them of the value they got.

08

Advocacy

Loving it →
Sharing it

◎ Goal

Share something they're proud of — their cat's identity, a good deal, a brand they believe in.

♥ Emotion

Pride and generosity. People share things that make them look good and help people they like.

△ Concerns

Feeling like an unpaid salesperson. Referral mechanics that feel spammy or transactional cheapen the pride.

✓ Desired outcome

Sharing feels natural and flattering, not like a chore or a hustle — an extension of the pride they already feel.

⛨ Build trust

Make the shareable artifact (the Cat ID, the year-in-review) inherently proud-making. Referral rewards that benefit the friend too, so it feels like a gift, not a pitch. Never pressure.

✦ Delight

A beautiful, shareable Cat ID card and a gifting flow ("give a friend's cat a membership") that carries the same ceremony as the member's own welcome. Belonging that can be given away.

✕ Mistakes to avoid

Aggressive "REFER 5 FRIENDS" nagging. Rewards that only benefit the referrer (feels selfish to share). Making advocacy a task instead of a natural overflow of pride. Interrupting good moments to beg for shares.

09

Doubt, Lapse & Win-back

Drifting →
Returning

◎ Goal

Either stop paying for something unused, or be reminded why it mattered — and leave/return with dignity.

♥ Emotion

Fading engagement, mild guilt, or frustration. At cancellation: wanting it to be quick and painless.

△ Concerns

"Will they make cancelling a fight?" A hard exit turns a quiet lapse into a public grievance and a lost future member.

✓ Desired outcome

If they leave, they leave respected and might return. If they were wavering, an honest reminder of real value earns them back.

⛨ Build trust

Make cancelling genuinely easy and gracious — offer a pause instead of only "cancel." A dignified exit is the strongest reason a lapsed member comes back and recommends you anyway. Keep their Cat ID and records safe in case they return.

✦ Delight

An honest, warm win-back: "your cat's ID is still here, and here's what you'd be getting again." Preserve their history so returning feels like coming home, not starting over.

✕ Mistakes to avoid

Cancellation mazes, guilt-tripping, or hiding the button. Deleting their data/ID punitively. Desperate discount-spam that signals the product wasn't worth full price. Treating a leaver as an enemy instead of a future returner.

The two moments that decide everything

If resources are constrained, over-invest in exactly two stages: Stage 4 (Welcome & First Value) and Stage 6 (Redemption in the Wild). The first sets whether the member ever bonds; the second proves the product is real. Nail these two and the rest of the loop largely takes care of itself.

03

Part I · The First Five Minutes

Onboarding Strategy

Onboarding is where trust is won or forfeited and where most drop-off happens. The governing rule: ask for the least, deliver value the soonest, and postpone everything that isn't required to greet the member and charge them.

Every field is a small act of taking; the member has given you money and now wants you to give first. Design onboarding as a gift being handed over, not a form being processed.

What to request — and only this

The bar for any onboarding field is brutal: "Is this required to charge them, greet them, or issue the Cat ID right now?" If not, it waits. The minimum viable identity is small.

Ask now
The cat's name. This is the emotional core and the thing that makes the Cat ID feel real. It is the one field that gives more than it takes.
Ask now
The owner's name + one contact (phone or email). Enough to greet them, send the card, and reach them. Phone verification suits the Saudi market.
Ask now
Payment. Via a trusted, familiar method. As few fields as the processor allows — let Apple Pay / mada / STC Pay carry the details.
Ask now (optional, one tap)
A cat photo. Offered, never required. A photo dramatically increases attachment to the Cat ID — but it must be skippable in one tap and easy to add later.

What to never request

Some asks don't just add friction — they actively damage trust or signal the wrong intent. These are off-limits at onboarding, and most are off-limits entirely.

Never

National ID / Iqama number

Deeply sensitive in KSA, unnecessary for a pet membership, and instantly triggers "why do they need this?" Requesting it would nuke trust.

Never

Home address up front

Not needed to issue a digital card. Collect only if/when a physical item ships, and only then.

Never

Detailed medical history at signup

Valuable later, overwhelming now. It turns a joyful welcome into a vet intake form. Invite it gradually.

Never

Demographics, income, surveys

Nothing that feels like data-harvesting. Every non-essential question at the door reads as extraction, not care.

Challenge · "Collect it now while we have their attention."

The instinct to grab data during onboarding "while they're here" is exactly backwards. Attention at onboarding is fragile and expensive; spend it on delivering value, not extracting data. Data given after the member trusts you is more complete, more accurate, and freely offered. Rich cat profiles should be earned over weeks of goodwill, never demanded in minute one.

What can be postponed (and invited later)

These build a richer, stickier membership — but every one of them can wait until the member has already felt value. Invite them contextually, as enhancements, framed as benefits to the cat.

Breed, age, and weight (frame: "so we can send the right reminders"). Vaccination records and vet details (frame: "keep everything in one place / one-tap check-in"). Microchip number and emergency contact (frame: "so we can help if your cat is ever lost"). Additional cats. Home address (only when something ships). Notification and communication preferences. Each is offered as "want to add this?" — never as a blocking step.

How to reduce friction

Value before ask
Show the person their emergent Cat ID as they type the cat's name. Let them feel the payoff building before payment, so the ask feels justified.
Fewer fields, smarter defaults
Every removable field, removed. Every inferable value, inferred. Lean on native payment sheets, autofill, and single-tap options over typed forms.
One decision per step
Never present two questions at once. A clean, single-focus rhythm feels effortless; a dense form feels like work.
No dead ends
Every optional step has a visible, guilt-free skip. Every error is forgiving and explains the fix in plain words, next to the field.
Respect the payment moment
Keep the charge screen calm and certain: exact amount, exact next date, cancel path — nothing else competing for attention.
Never lose progress
If they drop off mid-flow, save what they entered and let them resume exactly where they left. Losing a half-finished signup is losing a paying member.

How to increase completion rates

Completion is an emotional outcome, not a UX trick. People finish what feels rewarding and safe.

Lever

Front-load the payoff

Deliver the emotional reward (the Cat ID reveal) as early as possible. The closer the reward, the higher the completion.

Lever

Show the finish line

Make it obvious the process is short and how much is left. Perceived length kills completion more than actual length.

Lever

Reassure at the risky step

At payment, place trust cues (security, cancel-anytime, exact terms) right where the anxiety spikes.

Lever

Celebrate the moment done

End on warmth and pride, not a plain confirmation — so the last feeling is "I'm glad I did this."

The onboarding north star

A person should be able to go from "curious" to "holding my cat's ID with pride" in under two minutes and under six inputs. If the flow can't hit that, cut scope until it can. Everything else is an enhancement invited later.

04

Part I · The Feeling of Belonging

Membership Experience

A subscription is a transaction. A membership is a relationship. The difference is entirely in how it feels — and Moracat must feel unmistakably like the second. This section defines the felt qualities the experience must carry.

People don't renew subscriptions; they renew identities. "I'm a Moracat member" should feel like a small, true statement about the kind of cat owner someone is.

Premium perception

Premium is not decoration — it's certainty, craft, and restraint. It's the feeling that competent, tasteful people made careful decisions on your behalf. It comes from what's absent as much as what's present.

Through restraint
Generous space, few elements, one clear action. Clutter reads as cheap; calm reads as expensive. The confidence to say less.
Through craft
Considered typography, smooth motion, precise copy, zero broken states. Premium is a thousand small things done right, felt as one seamless whole.
Through certainty
Nothing ever confuses, glitches, or surprises unpleasantly. Reliability is the most premium feeling of all.
Through care
The product notices things — the cat's name, birthday, a reminder that mattered. Being remembered is the essence of premium service.
Challenge · Premium vs. the discount mechanic.

There's a real tension: discounts feel transactional and coupon-y, which fights the premium feeling. Resolve it by reframing savings as recognition, not couponing. A member isn't "using a coupon"; a partner is "honouring a member." The saving is presented with the dignity of a members' rate, confirmed quietly, and tallied as accumulated care — never as flashing "DEAL!" badges. Same economics, opposite emotion.

Exclusivity — the right kind

Exclusivity does not mean hard to get. Moracat should be easy to join and feel special to belong to. The exclusivity is identity-based, not scarcity-based: you're inside a circle of people who take their cats seriously, and you carry a mark of it.

Signals of belonging: a unique Cat ID that's yours. Member-only rates and offers that non-members visibly don't get. Recognition by name. Access to a vetted network outsiders have to find on their own. The feeling that Moracat is "for people like me and my cat." Avoid false scarcity ("only 100 spots!") — it's manipulative and beneath the brand. Real exclusivity is about the quality of the circle, not the size of the door.

Convenience — the everyday proof

Convenience is where a membership earns its keep between emotional peaks. It's the quiet removal of friction from the cat-owner's life.

Always in reach

The Cat ID, benefits, and records available instantly — in the wallet, offline, one glance away. Never hunt for what you paid for.

The thinking, done for you

Reminders that arrive without being set. The right vet, already vetted. The next thing, already surfaced. Cognitive load lifted.

Effortless redemption

Using a benefit is a tap, not a negotiation. Convenience at the counter is the most persuasive feature there is.

Convenience is the rent a membership pays every single day; delight is the gift it gives on occasion.

Long-term value — compounding, not flat

A great membership gets better the longer you're in it. Design value to compound so leaving feels like losing something built up, not just switching off a service.

The Cat ID accrues history — records, milestones, a first-year, a photo timeline. Savings accumulate into a visible, satisfying total. The product learns the cat and gets more useful over time. Longevity earns recognition (a quiet anniversary acknowledgement, not a points scheme). By year two, a member isn't paying for discounts — they're maintaining an identity and a record they don't want to abandon. That is the moat.

Renewal motivation

The best renewal is the one the member never questions — because the value is obvious and the trust is total. Motivation to renew is built continuously, not at the renewal moment.

Accumulated value
By renewal, they can see what they saved and gained. The math makes renewal feel like keeping a good decision, not making a new risky one.
Sunk identity
Their cat's ID, history, and records live here. Renewing preserves an identity; cancelling abandons one. This is the strongest, most ethical retention force.
Trust dividend
A year of honest billing and real care means renewal carries no anxiety. Trust removes the friction that makes people cancel "just in case."
Forward pull
A gentle sense of what's coming next — new partners, a new season, the cat's next milestone. A reason to stay for the future, not just the past.
05

Part I · The Signature Artifact

The Cat ID Strategy

The Cat ID is Moracat's soul made tangible — the one artifact that turns an abstract subscription into a possession people are proud of. Treated as decoration, it's a gimmick. Treated as identity with a job to do, it becomes the reason the whole thing works.

The membership card is the product you can hold. Everything else Moracat does is invisible; the Cat ID is where belonging becomes something a person can see, carry, use, and show.

The central reframe · from vanity card to indispensable identity.

A digital card that only displays a number and a discount will be admired once and forgotten. An unused artifact is a cancellation waiting to happen. The strategic move is to give the Cat ID real jobs the owner genuinely needs — so it earns a place in daily life. The four jobs below transform it from "nice" to "can't cancel."

The four jobs of the Cat ID

Job 1 · Safety

Bring my cat home

If a cat is lost, the ID becomes a recovery tool — a scannable identity linking finder to owner, with microchip and contact details. This single use-case can justify the entire membership emotionally. For a cat owner, "we'll help bring your cat home" is worth more than any discount.

Job 2 · Health

Hold my cat's record

The ID is the home for vaccination history, vet notes, weight, and reminders — the thing owners are always losing track of. One-tap check-in at partner clinics with the record attached. Now the ID is used at every vet visit, for years.

Job 3 · Value

Unlock my member rate

The ID is the redemption token — show or tap it at partners for member pricing. Frictionless benefit access at the counter, working offline, confirming the saving after. The daily proof that membership pays.

Job 4 · Identity

Say who my cat is

A beautiful, personal object bearing the cat's name, photo, and unique number — a source of pride, shareable, giftable. The emotional anchor that makes all three practical jobs feel like belonging rather than admin.

What the Cat ID represents

Symbolically, the Cat ID says three things at once: "your cat is real and recognised" (an identity, a name in the world, a number that's uniquely theirs); "your cat is cared for" (safety net, health record, a responsible owner's mark); and "you belong" (proof of membership in a circle that takes cats seriously). It is equal parts passport, health card, membership badge, and keepsake. Design it to feel like all four.

How important it should feel

It should feel like the most valuable thing the membership gives — more central than any discount. The mental hierarchy for a member should be: "Moracat is where my cat's identity lives, and it saves me money," never the reverse. The ID is the headline; savings are a supporting benefit. If a member describes Moracat to a friend and mentions the discounts first, the design has failed to elevate the ID.

Ceremony, not issuance

Receiving the Cat ID must be a moment, not a transaction. Reveal it with a small sense of occasion — the name, the number, the cat becoming "official." First impressions of the ID set the emotional value of the entire membership. Never let it appear as a bland row in a settings screen.

How members should interact with it

Interaction should be frequent, effortless, and rewarding. The ID earns habit by being genuinely useful in small ways: glanced at with a flicker of pride, tapped to redeem at a store, shown at the vet to check in, opened to log or check a record, added to the phone wallet for instant access, and occasionally shared or gifted. The more natural reasons to open it, the more the membership stays present in the member's life — and presence is the antidote to churn.

When it should be shown

At the peak moment
The instant after joining — the reveal. The single most important appearance; the emotional foundation of the membership.
At the point of use
At partner clinics and stores for check-in and redemption. Must be reachable in one action and work without signal.
At home base
Prominent whenever the member opens Moracat — the ID (or its essence) is the front door, not buried in a menu.
In the wallet
Available as a phone-wallet pass, so it's accessible without even opening the app — the ultimate convenience.
In moments of pride
When sharing, gifting, or at milestones (birthday, anniversary, first year) — occasions to bring the ID forward warmly.
In an emergency
When a cat is lost — the ID becomes the recovery tool, instantly and without friction, exactly when it matters most.

How it builds trust

The ID is proof the member got something real for their money — a tangible artifact defeats the "what am I even paying for?" doubt that sinks subscriptions. Its usefulness in high-stakes moments (a lost cat, a vet visit) proves Moracat shows up when it counts, which is the deepest form of trust. And because it holds sensitive health data, how respectfully that data is handled becomes a daily, visible demonstration of Moracat's stewardship. The ID is where trust is both earned and displayed.

How it becomes part of daily membership

Daily relevance is engineered by giving the ID standing jobs and gentle, useful reasons to return — never by nagging. A reminder that opens to the record. A member rate that's used on the weekly pet-store run. A wallet pass glanced at in passing. A birthday that surfaces the ID with warmth. The goal is a membership that feels quietly on — woven into the ordinary rhythm of caring for a cat, so that by the time renewal arrives, cancelling the ID is unthinkable because it has become part of how the owner looks after their animal.

Beyond the brief · the physical Cat ID as a premium moment

Consider offering an optional physical Cat ID — a beautifully made card or a collar tag with the cat's name and number — as a delight upgrade or gift. Amex proved a physical artifact can carry enormous identity weight. A tangible tag also strengthens the lost-cat recovery use-case in the real world. Kept optional and premium, it deepens attachment without adding required friction — and becomes a natural gifting product.

Part II · The Permission Slip

Trust & Credibility

Trust is the currency Moracat spends on every ask — payment, data, health records, referrals. It is earned slowly and lost instantly. This section is the discipline that protects it, across seven fronts.

In this category, one broken promise costs more than ten kept ones earn. Design as if every member is quietly asking "should I trust these people?" — because they are.

Transparency

Transparency is not a policy page; it's a default posture. The member should never have to dig for the truth, and should never be surprised by it.

No hidden anything
Costs, terms, renewal dates, and what's included are stated plainly, up front, in the member's own language. If a benefit has limits, the limits are shown with the benefit — not in fine print.
Honest coverage
If there are few partners in someone's city, say so and show what's coming. Overpromising coverage is the fastest way to a betrayed member. Honesty about gaps builds more trust than pretending they don't exist.
Plain language
No legalese, no dark-pattern phrasing, no "by continuing you agree to…" buried consent. Say what's true in words a person actually understands.

Pricing clarity

Money is the highest-anxiety part of any subscription, and the single biggest driver of refunds, disputes, and distrust in the Saudi market where auto-renew wariness runs high. Over-communicate here.

Rule

Show the whole truth

Exact price, exact billing cycle, exact next-charge date — before they pay and always available after. No surprise totals at the last step.

Rule

Warn before every charge

Notify ahead of each renewal, never after. A charge a member forgot about is a refund request and a lost customer. A heads-up is a trust deposit.

Rule

Cancel is always visible

State "cancel anytime" plainly and make the path easy. Hiding it converts quiet churn into public anger and chargebacks.

Rule

Frame value, not just price

Anchor the fee against tangible value ("often covered in one vet visit") so the price feels like an obvious yes, honestly earned.

Privacy

Moracat holds two sensitive things: the member's contact/payment details and the cat's health data. Both must be treated as if the member is watching how you handle them — because trust in privacy is felt, not read.

Collect the minimum. State plainly what's collected and why, in benefit terms ("we keep vaccination dates so we can remind you"). Never share or sell member or cat data — and say so clearly, as a promise, not buried in a policy. Give members visible control: see it, edit it, export it, delete it. In KSA, respect PDPL expectations and cultural sensitivity around personal information. Privacy handled visibly well becomes a feature people notice and trust.

Security

Security must be real and feel real — but through competence, not theatre. Use trusted, familiar payment methods so members never hand raw card details to an unknown party. Protect accounts and health data properly. Communicate security in calm, concrete terms at the moments anxiety peaks (payment, sharing records) — not as a scattering of decorative padlock icons that erode credibility by trying too hard. The most reassuring security signal is a product that simply never feels risky to use.

Membership confidence

Beyond trusting the company, the member needs to feel confident in their own decision to join — that they didn't make a mistake. Confidence is built by proof, not persuasion.

Tangible proof of value
The Cat ID and visible savings prove "I got something real." A concrete artifact defeats buyer's remorse better than any reassurance.
Early wins
Deliver a real benefit fast — the sooner a member experiences value, the more confident and committed they become. Front-load the payoff.
Reliable competence
Nothing breaks, everything works, help is fast and human. Consistent reliability quietly compounds into deep confidence over months.
A safe exit
Paradoxically, knowing they can leave easily makes members more confident to stay. Freedom removes the "what if I'm trapped?" fear that drives premature cancellation.

Partner credibility

Partners are the product — and its biggest risk.

A membership's credibility is capped by its worst partner. If Moracat refers a member to a clinic that treats their cat poorly, or a store that doesn't honour the member rate, the damage lands on Moracat, not the partner. Curate ruthlessly — quality over quantity, every time. Brief partners so their staff recognise and honour the ID instantly. Give members a way to flag a bad experience, and act on it. Better to have five excellent partners than fifty unvetted ones. Every partner is Moracat wearing someone else's uniform.

Customer reassurance

Reassurance is trust delivered at the exact moment of doubt. Anticipate where anxiety spikes and place calm, human reassurance right there: at payment (safe, cancellable, exact terms), at first use (how it works, what to expect), at redemption (it'll work, here's how), when sharing data (why, and it's safe), and when something goes wrong (fast, warm, competent help that fixes it and follows up). Reassurance isn't a support page you visit; it's a feeling the product proactively provides, precisely when it's needed.

The trust ledger

Treat trust as an account with a running balance. Every honest disclosure, kept promise, and proactive bit of care is a deposit; every surprise charge, hidden term, broken redemption, or bad partner is a large withdrawal. Deposits are small and slow; withdrawals are sudden and huge. Design to keep the balance always climbing, because renewal is simply the moment the member checks their balance.

Part II · Staying for Years

Retention Strategy

Retention is not a feature you add; it's the accumulated result of every emotion the product created. This section is deliberately about psychology, not mechanics — because members don't stay for features, they stay for how the membership makes them feel about themselves and their cat.

The question that governs retention isn't "how do we stop them leaving?" It's "how do we make staying the obvious, effortless, emotionally-right thing to do?"

Retention is built long before renewal

By the time a renewal notice appears, the stay-or-go decision is largely already made — in the hundreds of small moments before it. A member who felt recognised, saved real money, was never surprised by a charge, and built up a record of their cat will renew almost without thinking. A member who felt ignored, saw abstract value, and got a silent charge will cancel almost without thinking. Retention is a lagging indicator of the whole experience. Fix retention upstream, not at the exit.

The psychological forces that keep members

Identity & belonging
The strongest force. "I'm a Moracat member" becomes part of how the person sees themselves as a good cat owner. People don't cancel their identity. Reinforce belonging constantly and gently.
Accumulated investment
Records, history, savings totals, milestones — the member has built something here. Leaving means abandoning it. The more they've accrued, the more staying feels like protecting an asset. (Use this ethically, by making the accrual genuinely valuable — never as a hostage.)
Habit & presence
A membership woven into weekly routine (the vet visit, the store run, the reminder) is barely a decision anymore. Habits are stickier than intentions. Earn small, recurring reasons to engage.
Trust & safety
A year of honest billing and real care means the member has no reason to flee "just in case." Removing anxiety removes the impulse to cancel defensively.
Care received
Feeling looked-after creates reciprocity and loyalty. A member who's been genuinely helped feels a bond that a spreadsheet of features can't explain.
Loss aversion, gently
People fear losing what they have more than they value gaining something new. Make it clear (kindly) what would be lost — the ID, the record, the rates — without ever weaponising it.
Members don't renew a service. They decline to abandon an identity they've quietly built.

The forces that make members leave

Understand the churn drivers to design them out. Members leave when: value goes invisible (they can't feel what they're paying for), a charge surprises them (broken trust, the classic cancel trigger), a key moment fails (a redemption that didn't work, a bad partner), the product goes silent (out of sight, out of mind, out of subscription), it nags (over-notification breeds resentment and muting), or life changes (the cat passes, finances tighten — handle these with grace, not desperation). Every one of these is preventable or softenable by design.

Retention by making value undeniable

The most important, most ethical retention lever is simply making the real value visible. A member who can see "you've saved SAR 420 and used 9 benefits this year" doesn't need to be convinced to stay — the evidence convinces them. Surface cumulative savings. Recap benefits used. Remember moments of care given. Turn the renewal notice into a proud year-in-review rather than a bill. If the value is real, the job of retention is mostly to reveal it. If the value isn't real, no retention tactic will save it — and that's a product problem, not a design one.

Retain by deserving it

The only durable retention strategy is to be worth keeping and make that obvious. Reject every retention "trick" that survives on friction, confusion, or fear — hidden cancel buttons, guilt walls, dark patterns. They boost a metric this quarter and destroy the brand's trust over years. A member kept by manipulation is a detractor in waiting. A member kept by genuine value is an advocate for life.

Beyond the brief · offer "pause," not just "cancel"

Many cancellations are temporary — a tight month, travel, a life event. Offering a graceful pause (keep the Cat ID and records, stop billing, resume anytime) retains the relationship without extracting money the member can't justify right now. It's honest, it's kind, and it converts a permanent loss into a temporary gap. Pausing preserves the accumulated identity that brings them back.

People are not rational subscribers optimising a spreadsheet. They are emotional creatures seeking belonging, avoiding loss, fearing being fooled, and quietly wanting to feel like good owners. Design for that person.

Six psychological principles to encode everywhere

Principle

Peak–end rule

People judge an experience by its emotional peak and its ending. Engineer a high peak (the Cat ID reveal) and warm endings to every flow — including cancellation.

Principle

Loss aversion

Losing hurts ~twice as much as gaining feels good. Make accumulated value (records, savings, identity) visible so leaving registers as a loss — never weaponised, only revealed.

Principle

Endowment effect

People value what feels like theirs. The Cat ID, named and personalised, becomes "my cat's identity" — owned, not rented. Personalisation is attachment.

Principle

Reciprocity

Give first — care, reminders, warmth — and asks feel fair. A membership that gives before it takes earns loyalty that price alone never buys.

Principle

Cognitive ease

Effort and confusion feel like risk; fluency feels like trust. The easier something is to do and understand, the more people like and believe it.

Principle

Identity consistency

People act in line with how they see themselves. Help members adopt the identity "I'm a devoted cat owner / a Moracat member," and staying becomes self-expression.

120 UX Rules for Moracat

The brief asked for at least one hundred. Here are 120, grouped into twelve clusters and written as one-line imperatives a designer, writer, or engineer can act on today. They are opinionated on purpose — a rule that could mean anything changes nothing.

Filter by cluster to focus. Every rule is numbered (R001–R120) so it can be cited in a ticket, a review, or a design critique. When two rules conflict, the one that protects trust wins.

Filter Showing all 120 rules
A

Cognitive Load & Clarity

Every screen should answer three questions instantly: where am I, what can I do, what happens next. Confusion reads as risk.

  • R001Show one primary action per screen; demote everything else to secondary or tertiary weight.
  • R002Never present more than five to seven choices at one decision point; group or progressively disclose the rest.
  • R003Lead every screen with a plain-language title that names the outcome, not the feature.
  • R004Prefer recognition over recall — surface options rather than asking members to remember them.
  • R005State the benefit before the mechanism; people commit to outcomes, not steps.
  • R006Use whitespace as a feature — a calm screen signals a premium, trustworthy product.
  • R007Cap body copy at ~70 characters per line so it stays effortless to read.
  • R008Make the most important number on any screen the largest thing on it.
  • R009Kill every field, tap, and sentence that doesn't change the member's decision.
  • R010If a screen needs a tutorial to be understood, redesign the screen — not the tutorial.
B

Onboarding & Friction

The goal of onboarding is not to collect data — it is to deliver the first moment of value before doubt sets in.

  • R011Deliver a visible win — the Cat ID — before asking for anything optional.
  • R012Ask only for what issuing the membership genuinely requires; postpone the rest to context.
  • R013Never request National ID, Iqama, or passport numbers — a cat membership has no reason to hold them.
  • R014Show a step count or progress bar so members always know how much is left.
  • R015Pre-fill everything you can (city, dialing code, payment wallet) and let members correct, not enter.
  • R016Make the cat's name the first thing you ask — it's emotional, easy, and sets the tone.
  • R017Let people explore the benefit before committing; gate value behind trust, not behind signup walls.
  • R018Explain why beside any field that feels personal — a one-line reason removes the flinch.
  • R019Never force account creation before showing what membership looks like.
  • R020Treat photo upload as a delight, never a requirement — a placeholder cat should look intentional and warm.
C

Money, Payment & Trust

This is where memberships live or die. Every ambiguity about money is read as a trap. Radical clarity is the strategy.

  • R021State the full price, the cycle, and the renewal date on one line, in plain words, before payment.
  • R022Never hide the total behind a "start" button — the number people pay is the number they must see first.
  • R023Show the cancel and pause path before asking for card details, not buried after.
  • R024Email a receipt and a plain-language summary the instant a charge succeeds.
  • R025Warn before every renewal charge — a surprise charge is the fastest way to lose trust and earn a chargeback.
  • R026Support mada, Apple Pay, and STC Pay — locally trusted rails lower the fear of entering a card.
  • R027Show trust cues at the payment step (secure badge, refund terms) exactly where anxiety peaks.
  • R028Say what happens at renewal, not just that it renews — amount, date, and how to stop it.
  • R029If a price ever changes, tell every existing member first, in advance, and in their own words.
  • R030Make refunds and disputes feel safe to raise — a fair, fast resolution buys more loyalty than a perfect flow.
D

The Cat ID & Identity

The Cat ID is the product's soul. It must feel earned, useful, and personal — never a decorative gimmick.

  • R031Reveal the Cat ID as a small ceremony — a moment of pride, not a silent database entry.
  • R032Give every Cat ID a unique, human-readable number the owner can read aloud with pride.
  • R033Make the ID do real jobs — vet check-in, lost-cat recovery, redemption — so it's used, not just admired.
  • R034Let the card live in Apple Wallet / Google Wallet so it's one swipe away, always.
  • R035Personalise the card with the cat's name, photo, and join date — details create ownership.
  • R036Make the card work offline — it must render at a vet counter with no signal.
  • R037Offer a scannable code on the ID that a finder or a partner can use in seconds.
  • R038Let members share the card image proudly — it is your best organic marketing.
  • R039Offer a physical Cat ID tag as an optional keepsake — the tangible object deepens the bond.
  • R040Never let the ID feel fake — if it can't yet do a job, don't claim the job; earn the claim.
E

Making Value Visible

A subscription's greatest enemy is invisibility. If members can't see what they're getting, they conclude they're getting nothing.

  • R041Keep a running, cumulative tally of money saved — the single most powerful anti-churn number you own.
  • R042Confirm the exact amount saved at the moment each benefit is used.
  • R043Compare savings-to-date against the price paid, so the membership visibly pays for itself.
  • R044Surface unused benefits gently — "you haven't tried this yet" recovers dormant value.
  • R045Send a periodic value recap (monthly nudge, annual review) — remembered value renews.
  • R046Translate abstract perks into concrete outcomes ("free grooming" → "a clean, calm cat").
  • R047Show what's new since a member last opened the app so it always feels alive.
  • R048Make the home screen a value dashboard, not a marketing billboard.
  • R049Count non-monetary value too — reminders honoured, records kept, a cat's history preserved.
  • R050Never let a month pass with zero evidence of value delivered; silence is perceived as waste.
F

Partner & Redemption Experience

The redemption counter is the moment of truth. The partner is the product in that instant — curate and orchestrate it ruthlessly.

  • R051Curate partners on quality, not quantity — one embarrassing partner taints the whole membership.
  • R052Make redemption a single, obvious action — show the ID, tap, done. No fumbling.
  • R053Ensure partner staff can recognise and honour the membership instantly — train the counter, not just the app.
  • R054Show partner offers on a map with honest, city-level coverage — never imply reach you don't have.
  • R055State each offer's terms up front — expiry, limits, exclusions — so nobody is embarrassed at the till.
  • R056Give a graceful fallback when a partner fails to honour an offer — recover the member's dignity immediately.
  • R057Let members rate a redemption so you can prune weak partners with evidence.
  • R058Confirm redemption visibly to both sides — a shared "done" state removes doubt and disputes.
  • R059Present partners as curated recommendations from a trusted friend, not as ad inventory.
  • R060Design the counter interaction for a noisy, one-handed, low-signal real-world moment.
G

Retention, Renewal & Lifecycle

Retention is not a save-offer at the exit — it is a hundred small proofs of value delivered long before renewal day.

  • R061Earn renewal across the whole year, not with a discount panic in the final week.
  • R062Offer pause, not only cancel — a paused member is a kept relationship; a cancelled one is a loss.
  • R063Make cancellation honest and one-tap — a dignified exit is the strongest reason to return.
  • R064Preserve the cat's records after cancellation so returning feels like coming home, not starting over.
  • R065Celebrate the membership anniversary with a year-in-review the owner wants to keep and share.
  • R066Recognise and reward tenure — longstanding members should feel their status, not just repay it.
  • R067Detect the drift toward churn (no logins, no redemptions) and re-engage with value, not guilt.
  • R068Never guilt-trip a leaver ("are you sure you want to abandon your cat?") — manipulation poisons advocacy.
  • R069Win back with a genuine reason to return — new partners, restored value — not a desperate coupon.
  • R070Make renewal a proud recommitment moment, framed by everything the past year delivered.
H

Micro-interactions, Motion & Feedback

Polish is not decoration — responsive, tasteful feedback is how an interface earns the word "premium."

  • R071Acknowledge every tap within ~100ms — instant feedback is the baseline of feeling trustworthy.
  • R072Keep transitions in the 150–300ms range — fast enough to feel responsive, slow enough to feel considered.
  • R073Reserve your richest animation for the Cat ID reveal — spend delight where it converts to memory.
  • R074Use motion to explain, not to impress — animation should clarify what changed and where it went.
  • R075Always honour prefers-reduced-motion — accessibility and calm over spectacle.
  • R076Show progress for anything over a second — a skeleton or spinner turns waiting into patience.
  • R077Make success states feel warm — a small, tasteful moment of celebration, never a confetti cannon.
  • R078Give buttons real pressed, loading, and done states so actions never feel ignored.
  • R079Use subtle haptics at meaningful moments (issued, redeemed) where the platform allows.
  • R080Never animate the same element twice for no reason — restraint is what separates premium from busy.
I

Content, Voice & Language

Words are interface. The voice is warm, composed, and quietly premium — a knowledgeable friend, never a coupon barker.

  • R081Write like a trusted friend who knows cats — warm, plain, and never salesy.
  • R082Use the cat's name in copy wherever you can — it turns a UI into a relationship.
  • R083Replace jargon and legalese with plain language everywhere a human will read it.
  • R084Make every error message say what happened and exactly what to do next.
  • R085Never shout discounts — frame savings as a benefit of belonging, not the reason to belong.
  • R086Label buttons with the action they perform ("Issue my Cat ID"), never vague verbs ("Submit," "OK").
  • R087Keep a consistent lexicon — a "member," a "Cat ID," a "benefit" — and never drift between synonyms.
  • R088Front-load the point of every sentence; nobody reads to the end of a paragraph on a phone.
  • R089Let the brand's warmth toward cats show — this affection is the product's true differentiator.
  • R090Proofread in both languages by a native speaker — one clumsy phrase undermines the premium promise.
J

Accessibility & Inclusivity

A premium membership is one everyone can use with dignity. Accessibility is not a checklist — it is respect made visible.

  • R091Meet at least 4.5:1 text contrast — legibility is the first courtesy of a trustworthy interface.
  • R092Give every touch target at least ~44×44px — thumbs, not styluses, use this product.
  • R093Never carry meaning by colour alone — pair it with text, icon, or shape.
  • R094Support dynamic type — the layout must survive a member doubling the font size.
  • R095Label every control and image for screen readers — the Cat ID must be announced, not silent.
  • R096Make the entire flow completable one-handed — most sessions happen on the move.
  • R097Ensure visible focus states for keyboard and switch-control users.
  • R098Don't rely on hover — the primary device is a touchscreen with no hover state.
  • R099Test with real assistive technology, not just an automated audit.
  • R100Design for one-handed reach zones — keep primary actions in the lower third of the screen.
K

Bilingual, Cultural & Saudi Market

Beyond the brief, but decisive for this market. Moracat is a Saudi product for Saudi cat owners — it must feel born here, not translated into here.

  • R101Design Arabic-first and RTL-native — Arabic is the default experience, not a mirrored afterthought.
  • R102Let members switch language instantly, at any point, without losing their place.
  • R103Choose an Arabic typeface with the same care as the Latin one — Arabic must look premium, not default.
  • R104Mirror layouts, icons, and motion direction correctly for RTL — a lazy flip reads as foreign.
  • R105Honour local payment habits (mada, STC Pay, Apple Pay) as first-class, not fallback, options.
  • R106Comply with PDPL — store data in-region, minimise collection, and say so plainly to build trust.
  • R107Time notifications around prayer times and local rhythms — respect for context is respect for the member.
  • R108Draw on the affection for cats in Arab and Islamic culture — it is the emotional soul of this brand.
  • R109Support the household, not just the individual — families and majlis circles share care of a cat.
  • R110Localise real content — SAR pricing, Saudi cities, Hijri-aware dates — never a token currency swap.
L

Errors, Emptiness & Edge States

Trust is built in the unhappy path. How the product behaves when things go wrong is what members actually remember.

  • R111Design the empty state as a first impression — a new member's home is never truly empty, it's a welcome.
  • R112Turn every error into a recovery — say what went wrong, why, and the one tap that fixes it.
  • R113Never blame the member for a failure — the system's tone stays calm and takes responsibility.
  • R114Handle no-signal gracefully — the Cat ID and core value must survive an offline vet visit.
  • R115Prevent errors before catching them — sensible defaults and validation beat apologetic dialogs.
  • R116Confirm destructive actions (cancel, delete) clearly, but never trap people to prevent them.
  • R117Preserve entered data through any interruption — a dropped connection must never erase progress.
  • R118Show a payment failure honestly and offer an immediate, dignified retry — don't lock the member out.
  • R119Give every loading state a purpose ("issuing your Cat ID…") so waiting feels like progress.
  • R120Make the support path always one tap away — reachable help is itself a feature of trust.
Don't ship all 120 at once

A rulebook is a compass, not a launch checklist. Trying to satisfy every rule before shipping guarantees you ship nothing. Use Section 11's priority tiers to decide the order — the Critical rules make or break trust and belong in v1; the rest earn their way in as the product matures.

Section 10 · Stolen With Pride

10

Best Practices Worth Stealing

The best membership and subscription products in the world have already spent billions learning what earns loyalty. Here is what each does exceptionally, and precisely how Moracat should adapt it — never copy the mechanic, steal the principle beneath it.

Each card names the company, the single lesson to take, and its translation into a Moracat decision. The through-line: great memberships sell identity and belonging, and let savings arrive as proof.

Identity & the physical object

Apple — Apple One

Make the object feel inevitable

Apple wraps hardware, packaging, and Wallet passes in a ceremony that makes ownership feel special. For Moracat: treat the Cat ID reveal and the Wallet pass as the "unboxing" moment — the digital object should feel as considered as an Apple box.

American Express

The card is the status

Amex turned a payment card into an identity people display. Members stay for what the card says about them. For Moracat: the Cat ID is the equivalent artifact — make it beautiful, shareable, and quietly prestigious, and offer a physical tag for members who want the tangible symbol.

Costco

Membership legitimises the price

People trust Costco's low prices because they paid to get in — the fee reframes savings as an earned privilege, not a gimmick. For Moracat: position partner discounts as a members-only privilege, which makes them feel premium rather than cheap, and makes the fee feel like the key to a club.

Sephora — Beauty Insider

Tiers people want to climb

A free, visible tier ladder gives members a status to earn and protect. For Moracat: when the base membership is proven, introduce tenure or engagement tiers that recognise devoted owners — status is a renewal reason that costs nothing to deliver.

Value made visible

Amazon Prime

Bundle until leaving feels costly

Prime layers shipping, video, and perks so members can't mentally separate what they'd lose. For Moracat: accumulate visible, varied value — savings, records, reminders, identity — so cancelling registers as losing many things at once, not one.

Spotify — Wrapped

Give data back as a gift

Wrapped turns a year of usage into a shareable, emotional story — free marketing and a loyalty spike every December. For Moracat: build a "Cat's Year in Review" — savings, milestones, care moments — that owners are proud to share and that lands right before renewal.

Uber One

Show the saving at the moment

Uber One states "you saved X on this order" at checkout, making an invisible subscription tangible on every use. For Moracat: confirm the exact riyals saved at each redemption and keep a lifetime tally — the running number is your strongest retention asset.

IKEA Family

Free belonging that still feels special

A no-cost membership with members-only prices and perks builds a huge, loyal base and habituates the swipe. For Moracat: consider a free identity tier (the Cat ID itself) that delivers belonging first — then let paid benefits deepen a relationship that already exists.

Emotion, care & the pet bond

Chewy

Care that expects nothing back

Chewy sends handwritten cards, surprise pet portraits, and flowers when a pet dies — unscalable warmth that creates fanatical loyalty and word of mouth. For Moracat: this is the north star. Remember the cat's name and birthday; mark milestones; respond to loss with humanity. Reciprocity, not discounts, is the moat.

Calm / Peloton

Sell an identity, track a streak

Members don't buy features — they become "a person who meditates," "a runner." Streaks and gentle accountability reinforce that self-image. For Moracat: help members adopt the identity "a devoted cat owner." Reflect their care back to them so membership becomes self-expression, and cancelling feels off-brand for who they are.

Disney+ / Netflix

A reason to return on a cadence

Regular new content creates a rhythm of re-engagement that keeps the subscription alive between big moments. For Moracat: establish a predictable cadence — new partners, seasonal offers, care tips — so the app is worth opening monthly, not just at renewal.

Sam's Club — Scan & Go

Remove friction at the moment of truth

Scan & Go lets members skip the checkout line entirely — the perk people feel every single visit. For Moracat: make redemption at the counter frictionless and fast; the smoothness of that ten-second interaction is what members will describe to their friends.

The pattern beneath all of them

Notice what none of these leaders lead with: a bigger discount. They lead with identity (Amex, Calm), unscalable care (Chewy), visible proof of value (Uber One, Spotify), and frictionless moments of truth (Sam's Club). Discounts are table stakes; belonging is the product. Moracat's advantage is that its subject — a beloved cat — is already the most emotional bond a subscription could be built on. Most brands manufacture that feeling. Moracat only has to honour it.

Section 11 · What To Build, In What Order

11

Prioritised Recommendations

A strategy is only as good as its sequencing. These recommendations are sorted into four tiers by impact on trust, activation, and retention — the three things that decide whether Moracat survives its first year. Build the Critical tier before anything else; it is the foundation everything above rests on.

Each tier opens with the question it answers. Within a tier, order is roughly the order to build. Resist the pull of the shiny Future items — trust and first value are what turn a signup into a member.

Critical — Non-negotiable for v1

Trust & first value

Answers: "Can a stranger trust us with their money and their cat in the first five minutes?" Without these, nothing else matters — members never form.

Radical payment & renewal transparencyFull price, cycle, renewal date, and cancel path shown before payment; receipt and renewal warnings always. This single discipline prevents the chargebacks and betrayal that kill young subscriptions.
Arabic-first, RTL-native experienceNot a translation layer — the default, premium-quality Arabic experience with instant language switching. This is a Saudi product; it must feel born here.
Instant digital Cat ID issuance with ceremonyThe first moment of value, delivered before any optional ask. The emotional peak that converts a signup into a member proud of their cat's identity.
A Cat ID that does at least one real jobShip lost-cat recovery (scannable code) and/or one-tap vet check-in from day one. Utility is what separates an identity from a gimmick.
Wallet pass + offline cardThe card must live in Apple/Google Wallet and render with no signal at a vet counter. Availability at the moment of truth is the product working.
Cumulative savings visibilityConfirm riyals saved at each redemption and keep a lifetime tally. The number that makes an invisible subscription feel indispensable.
Ruthless partner curation + one-tap redemptionA small set of quality partners, a frictionless counter interaction, and staff who honour it instantly. In redemption, the partner is the product.
Honest, one-tap cancel & pauseA dignified exit that preserves records. Counter-intuitively, the easiest cancellation builds the trust that drives the most renewals and returns.

High — Shortly after launch

Deepen & retain

Answers: "Now that they've joined, how do we make the value undeniable and the membership personal?" These convert new members into renewing ones.

A savings & value dashboard as the home screenMake the home a running proof of value — saved, used, cared-for — not a marketing billboard. Directly attacks the invisibility that kills subscriptions.
Cat-profile personalisationName, photo, breed, birthday woven through the experience. Personalisation drives the endowment effect — "my cat's membership," owned not rented.
Partner map with honest city-level coverageShow real reach per city, never implied national coverage. Honesty about limits builds more trust than exaggerated scale.
Referral & gifting with the same ceremonyLet proud members share belonging; make a gifted membership arrive with the full welcome ritual. Advocacy is cheaper and stronger than acquisition ads.
Milestone & anniversary celebrationsMark the cat's join-date, first year, and care milestones. Peak-end moments that make renewal an emotional recommitment, not a silent charge.
Prayer-aware, well-timed notificationsContact that respects local rhythms and always carries value. Respect for context earns the right to keep notifying.
Optional physical Cat ID tagA tangible keepsake for members who want the object. Deepens the bond and doubles as real-world marketing on every collar.

Medium — As the base matures

Habit & expansion

Answers: "How do we turn a proven membership into a daily habit and a broader relationship?" Valuable, but only once the core loop is loved.

Care content & a reason to return monthlySeasonal tips, new-partner reveals, cat-care guidance. Establishes the cadence that keeps the app alive between renewals.
Gentle streaks & care habitsLight, non-nagging reinforcement of the "devoted owner" identity. Habit formation without the manipulation that breeds resentment.
Tiered plans & status recognitionReward tenure and engagement with earned status. A renewal reason that costs little to deliver and flatters loyal members.
Multi-cat & household supportMultiple Cat IDs per account and shared family access. Matches how Saudi households actually care for cats — collectively.
Community & owner-to-owner belongingLightweight ways for members to connect around their cats. Belonging to a community is stickier than belonging to a brand.

Future — The long horizon

Platform & ambition

Answers: "What could Moracat become once it owns the trusted cat identity in the Kingdom?" Powerful, but dangerous to chase before the core is beloved.

Concierge & premium servicesVet booking, grooming, sitting, and travel help built on the trusted identity. Expands value once the membership has earned the right to sell more.
Vet telehealth & digital health recordsThe Cat ID becomes a portable medical record and a route to remote care. Turns identity into indispensable infrastructure.
Insurance & financial-care tie-insPartner products underwritten on a known, verified cat identity. A natural monetisation of trust — introduced only once trust is unquestioned.
A curated marketplace & premium physical goodsEssentials and a Palm-and-Pearl-grade product line for members. The membership becomes a storefront people want to shop.
The identity layer for the Saudi cat economyEvery vet, store, and service recognising the Moracat Cat ID. The endgame: Moracat as the default passport every cat owner carries.
The sequencing principle

Trust before value, value before growth, growth before expansion. Each tier earns the right to the next. A Future-tier feature shipped on a foundation that hasn't earned trust doesn't accelerate Moracat — it exposes it. Build downward from Critical, and let the ambition of the Future tier be the reason to get the Critical tier exactly right.

Appendix A · Beyond the Brief

A

The Saudi Market Layer

The brief did not ask for this. It is included because a UX strategy for a Saudi cat membership that ignores its market would be professionally incomplete. This is the layer that makes Moracat feel of Saudi Arabia, not merely available in it.

Why this belongs in the strategy

Cats hold a genuinely warm place in Arab and Islamic culture — a rare case where a product's subject is already loved by the society it launches into. That affection is not a marketing angle to bolt on; it is the emotional foundation the entire membership should be built upon. Most brands spend fortunes manufacturing the feeling Moracat inherits for free.

Cultural soul
Let the brand's affection for cats echo their positive cultural and religious status. This is the differentiator no aggregator or discount app can copy — warmth toward the animal is the product's heart.
Arabic-first
Arabic is the default, premium experience — RTL-native, beautifully typeset, instantly switchable. Members should never feel they're using a foreign product that was translated as an afterthought.
Local payment trust
mada, STC Pay, and Apple Pay as first-class options. Familiar rails dramatically lower the fear of entering payment details with a new brand.
Privacy & PDPL
Store data in-region, collect the minimum, and say so plainly. In a market increasingly conscious of data rights, visible restraint is a trust advantage — and never ask for National ID or Iqama.
Rhythm & timing
Time communications around prayer times and local daily rhythms. Respect for when and how people live is respect for the member.
The household
Support families and majlis circles who share a cat's care — multiple carers, shared access, collective belonging. Membership here is rarely a solo act.
Vision 2030 fit
A homegrown, quality-of-life brand that dignifies pet care aligns naturally with the national direction — a story worth telling to partners and members alike.
The temptation to globalise too early

It is tempting to design a "market-neutral" product that could scale anywhere. Resist it. Winning Saudi Arabia deeply — being unmistakably the local cat membership — is a stronger foundation than being a generic one that fits everywhere and belongs nowhere. Depth first; breadth is a later chapter.

Appendix B · Knowing It's Working

B

Metrics & Guardrails

A strategy needs a way to know it's working — and guardrails to ensure the numbers are earned honestly, not gamed. Measure the emotional truths this document argues for, not just the vanity counts.

Activation

The first-value rate

% of signups who reach the issued Cat ID and their first real value moment. This — not raw signups — is the number that predicts everything downstream.

Redemption

The truth-moment success rate

% of attempted redemptions that succeed smoothly, and average members who redeem at least once a month. A membership that's never used is already churning.

Value realised

Savings-to-price ratio

Cumulative member savings versus fee paid. When this visibly crosses 1×, renewal becomes rational as well as emotional. Track how many members see it.

Retention

Renewal, pause, and return rates

Renewal rate, plus the share who pause rather than cancel, plus win-back returns. Pause and return are signs the dignified-exit strategy is working.

Advocacy

Referrals & shares per member

Cat IDs and year-in-reviews shared, and memberships gifted. Organic sharing is the clearest proof that belonging — not discount — is landing.

Trust

Chargebacks, complaints, support ease

Chargeback rate, billing complaints, and how quickly support resolves. Low numbers here mean the transparency discipline is genuinely working.

The guardrail

Never optimise a metric by breaking a promise in this document. Retention bought by making cancellation hard, activation inflated by forcing signups, or engagement driven by nagging — all move a number and destroy the trust the number was meant to represent. If improving a metric requires a dark pattern, the metric is wrong, not the principle. Measure the belonging; never fake it.

Signups are a rented number.
Belonging is the only one that compounds.
