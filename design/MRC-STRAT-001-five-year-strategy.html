A · Executive thesis

A replenishment business funds a registry. The registry is the company.

Saudi Arabia has roughly 1.7M owned cats in ~850k households Inference, spending about SAR 1.0–1.3B a year on food and litter Inference. It has only ~200–300 private veterinary clinics Fact (MEWA counted 196 in 2022), no national registry for resident cats Fact, and more than 1,000 pet stores competing on price Fact.

Selling cat food here is a commodity fight Moracat cannot win on its own. After VAT, the committed plans keep 13–16% contribution Fact (MRC-FIN-002). TwinCats already sells monthly bundles, vet booking and a "VIP membership" Fact. Amazon.sa already offers Subscribe & Save at 10% off Fact. A subscription box is not a moat.

What nobody in the Kingdom owns is the verified record of a cat's life: who the cat is, who its owner is, what vaccines it has had, which clinic gave them, what it eats, and what happened when it changed hands. That record has three properties a box lacks:

  • It is written by a small, finite set of clinics, so a network of ~200 of them can be captured.
  • It gets more valuable every year it accumulates, and cannot be copied by a later entrant because history can't be backfilled.
  • It becomes the expected document at every hand-over: adoption, breeder sale, boarding, travel, insurance.

The strategy: use the kitten's first year as the wedge, recurring essentials as the cash engine, and the clinic-written Cat ID as the moat.

Why the kitten year is the wedge. It is the one moment when an owner has no habits yet, the most questions, and a mandatory series of 3–4 clinic visits inside 16 weeks. Each visit is a chance for a clinic to write into a Moracat Cat ID. Moracat already has the only kitten tier in the Kingdom Fact.

What makes it hard to copy. It is not a data network effect, which is weak NFX. It is a local two-sided record network: every clinic that writes to Cat ID makes the ID more useful to every owner who ever visits a second clinic, boards the cat or re-homes it. Every cat with history makes Moracat more useful to every clinic. The goal is to become the CARFAX of cats — the history report a buyer expects before accepting a kitten.

The honest money. In the base case Moracat reaches ~11,000 member households, ~230k registered cats and ~200 connected clinics by Year 5, on SAR 35M revenue. It needs ~SAR 18M of cumulative funding and breaks even around month 60. That is a sturdy company, not yet a SAR-hundreds-of-millions one. The aggressive case (SAR 65M revenue, EBITDA-positive from month 50) gets there, and only if the registry is valued as infrastructure rather than as a store.

Where the strategy must be ruthless: overhead discipline and acquisition volume through clinics swing the outcome more than margin does (§E sensitivity). Never own clinics, never lock owners' data in, never chase GCC expansion before Saudi is won.

What the sizing means for Moracat Inference

The average Saudi cat eats ~SAR 44 of food a month. Moracat's average household box is ~SAR 350, which is premium-tail pricing.

  • Premium replenishment market: households in Riyadh, Makkah and the Eastern Province (68% of population) that spend ≥ SAR 200/month on cat essentials. That is roughly 70–105k households.
  • Mid-market autoship: another ~80–100k households at SAR 120–200/month.
  • Cat ID: addresses the whole ~1.7M-cat market, because it is free.

That asymmetry is the strategy: the box can only ever reach a niche; the record can reach the country.

Where they get vet care today. From an independent neighbourhood clinic chosen by proximity and word of mouth, with records kept on paper or in the clinic's own software. There is no cross-clinic history Fact: no registry; no Saudi practice-management vendor found. What owners lack: a vaccination schedule they can see, honest price benchmarks (MEWA itself surveyed vet prices), a portable history, and feeding guidance by life stage.

Why they abandon subscriptions McKinsey; Recurly:

  • More than 1/3 of cancellations happen within 3 months.
  • ~1/3 of churn is involuntary (failed payments).
  • Replenishment boxes keep 45% of subscribers at 12 months, ~10 points better than curation boxes.
  • Saudi-specific triggers Assumption: a wrong substitution, a late delivery in summer heat, a box that doesn't last the month (the Catmer failure mode), and the cat simply refusing the food.

White space — what nobody in Saudi Arabia does today
  • A persistent Cat ID, verified by clinics and tied to the microchip, that transfers with ownership.
  • Clinic-written records that change what arrives at the door: a weight trend adjusts the feeding plan, a renal diagnosis triggers a food switch with vet sign-off.
  • A true-month box with a kitten tier and per-cat scaling (already Moracat's pricing position).
  • Preventive-care plans sold by the clinic but administered on a shared rail. Petlove, Brazil's leading online pet retailer, reached 220k+ pets on health plans Fact.
  • A cat "history report" at adoption and breeder sale.

Why not simply the top score

Veterinary integration scores highest on moat criteria but is a bad wedge. It is a two-sided cold start:

  • Clinics won't adopt a system their patients don't bring.
  • Patients won't bring an ID their clinic doesn't read.
  • Clinics pay nothing, so it funds nothing.

Owner-held records apps that skipped the clinic side died: Pawprint became a content brand Fact. Recurring essentials is the revenue engine, but on its own it has no defensibility (Amazon S&S, TwinCats, Salla bundles).

The kitten year is the only wedge that contains the other two. Recommend

  1. The owner has no habits. No preferred store, brand or clinic yet, so switching cost from incumbents is near zero.
  2. Pain and information need peak. Food transitions, litter training, fear of illness.
  3. The vaccine series is mandatory. It forces 3–4 clinic visits in ~16 weeks. Each one is a natural moment for the clinic to write to a Cat ID and for Moracat to send the owner back to that clinic. That is how the atomic network forms: one clinic plus ~150–300 member cats within 5–10 km.
  4. Kittens enter through breeders, shelters and rescuers. That is a B2B2C channel with near-zero CAC, where a verified history adds sale or adoption value.
  5. The relationship lasts 12–15 years. Kitten 199 is priced as acquisition MRC-FIN-002, and graduation to Complete at ~9 months is built into the feeding engine.
The wedge's failure condition

The kitten tier earns ~4% VAT-era contribution. If fewer than 55% of kittens graduate to a paid adult plan or autoship by month 12, or fewer than 40% of kitten members have at least one clinic-written entry, the wedge is subsidising a store rather than building a registry. Kill it or re-price it.

C.1 The data that gains value with scale

Compounds (build these)

  • Clinic-authored vaccination and microchip records: the registry spine.
  • Weight-by-age curves by breed from real Saudi cats. No one has these, and they power feeding plans and early vet flags.
  • Ownership-transfer events (breeder → buyer, shelter → adopter).
  • Consumption per cat per month by food type: drives forecasting and supplier negotiation.
  • Life-stage transitions: kitten graduation, senior onset, neuter date.

Plateaus (collect, don't overvalue)

  • Flavour preferences and SKU affinity.
  • Community engagement and likes.
  • Demographic profile of owners.
  • Site browsing behaviour.
  • Aggregated "insights" sold to brands. Legally risky under PDPL, and it erodes trust.

C.2 Switching costs — the ethical line

Legitimate (earned convenience)

  • An auto-adjusting feeding plan that already knows the cat.
  • One place clinics, boarders and groomers read.
  • Reminders that route to the owner's own clinic.
  • Founding price-lock and tenure recognition.
  • Household multi-cat module pricing.

Forbidden (hostage lock-in)

  • Withholding record export or charging for it.
  • Making Cat ID access conditional on a paid plan.
  • Cancellation friction, "call to cancel", or hidden renewals.
  • Exclusivity clauses with clinics or breeders.
  • Forfeiting prepaid terms on cancel (flagged as a fire in the 2026-07-17 audit).

Ship full record export on day one (PDF plus a structured JSON format) Recommend. It sounds counter-intuitive. It removes the one argument a competitor or regulator could use against Moracat ("they hold your cat's history hostage"). It is the reason clinics can trust a third-party system: Vetsource and Covetrus both lead with "the practice owns its data" Fact. And it costs nothing, because the moat is that the record keeps getting written in Moracat, not that it is locked there.

C.6 The flywheel to obsess over

The north-star metric: clinic-verified active cats (CVAC). A cat counts if it has at least one clinic-authored entry in the last 13 months and an owner active in the last 90 days. A registration is not an asset. A cat whose record a clinic keeps writing, and whose owner keeps acting on it, is. Recommend

How the Cat ID becomes a passport Inference

Passports work because a trusted issuer vouches for the holder and many verifiers accept the document. For Cat ID:

  • Issuers: clinics (chip + vaccination), breeders (lineage + first vaccines), shelters (adoption).
  • Verifiers: other clinics, boarders, groomers, airlines and travel agents, residential compounds with pet rules, insurers, and eventually municipalities.

Moracat's job is to make issuing free and faster than paper and verifying one scan. The UK shows what goes wrong without this: 22 fragmented chip databases, 72% of chipped dogs with wrong details, 83% of those because the new keeper never updated them Battersea. An effortless ownership transfer is the single most important V5 feature.

G · Strategic priorities

The five obsessions

  1. Clinics writing records, district by district. Not clinics "signed", not QR stands placed. Entries written. Win three Riyadh districts to ≥60% density before touching a fourth. This is the moat, and a later entrant cannot backfill it.
  2. The kitten year as a product, end to end. From breeder or shelter hand-over, to the vaccine series written by the clinic, to feeding transitions, to the graduation ceremony. Measure graduation and clinic-entry rates weekly. If this loop works, everything else is scaling. If it doesn't, nothing else matters.
  3. Unit economics before geography. Close the −10% COGS gate, then the litter container, then private label. Keep overhead growth below gross-profit growth every quarter; the sensitivity analysis says bloat kills Moracat faster than competitors do.
  4. The perfect box and zero involuntary churn. Exact SKUs, lasting a real month, on time in 45°C summers, with dunning and pause built in. Replenishment only wins on reliability, and reliability is what makes the record-driven box credible.
  5. Neutral, portable, honest infrastructure. Free export, no exclusivity, no clinic ownership, no data sales, no dark renewal patterns. Neutrality is what lets competitors' clinics, breeders and eventually regulators use Moracat's rails. It is the one thing a well-funded retailer structurally cannot copy.

H.2 The anti-roadmap

Markets not to enter

  • Dogs, birds, aquatics, exotics, before Year 5, and probably ever.
  • GCC before Saudi gates are met (§J).
  • Livestock or falcons. Tempting in Saudi Arabia, but a different regulator, customer and business.
  • Nationwide coverage before district density.

Products not to sell

  • Broad toy and accessory catalogues; novelty boxes.
  • Prescription medicines (MEWA licensing, clinic conflict).
  • Own-brand food before SFDA registration and a reliable co-packer.
  • Live animals, or any breeder that can't pass verification.

Features not to build

  • A full practice-management system that competes with clinics' own software. Integrate instead.
  • Tele-vet diagnosis inside Moracat, which competes with the clinic channel.
  • AI that diagnoses. Flags only, routed to the vet.
  • A wallet, points currency or NFT-style Cat IDs.
  • A native app before the web portal retains.
  • Social feeds, DMs, marketplaces.

Customers not to chase

  • Deal-hunters who arrive through coupon aggregators.
  • Mass-market SAR 44/month households, with plans. Give them Cat ID, not boxes.
  • Large breeders wanting wholesale pricing without verification.
  • Corporates wanting white-label cat IDs under their own brand.

Partnerships to reject

  • Any clinic exclusivity, in either direction.
  • A distributor or brand deal that forbids stocking competitors.
  • Data-sharing deals with brands or insurers using identifiable data.
  • Buying or co-owning clinics.
  • Influencers without a Mawthooq licence, or who promote unethical breeding.

Growth tactics that damage the brand

  • Fake scarcity or "only 3 places left" (R006).
  • Hard-to-cancel renewals; forfeiting prepaid terms.
  • Cash-per-signup to vets.
  • Exploiting religious reverence for cats in ads.
  • Savings claims not backed by the market-basket ledger.

Revenue that distracts from the moat: grooming and boarding operations, events ticketing, merchandise, sponsored content from pet-food brands, B2B pet-food wholesale, and a general Salla-style marketplace. Each can be profitable, and each moves the team's attention away from the one thing a competitor can't copy.

Q · The founder's question

If you could build only one thing in the next 24 months

Build the clinic write-path for the kitten year in Riyadh. Concretely: a network of about 60 Riyadh clinics that write every kitten's vaccinations, microchip and weights into a Moracat Cat ID, fed by 30+ breeders and the major rescues issuing IDs at hand-over, and paying back every clinic by returning its patients through reminders.

Measure it with one number: clinic-verified active cats. Target: 8,000 by month 24, concentrated so that three to five districts pass 60% clinic density.

Why this and not the box, the brand or the app

  • It's the only asset that time protects. A competitor with SAR 100M can copy the box in a quarter, the app in six months and the brand in a year. It cannot copy three years of vaccination history for 70,000 cats, written by the clinics those owners trust. Records can't be backfilled.
  • It sits on a small, finite set of nodes. Saudi Arabia has ~200–300 private clinics, and Riyadh has a fraction of them. Winning a majority of a finite, slow-growing supply side is achievable for a small team. Winning a majority of 1.7M cat owners through ads is not.
  • It turns the commerce business from a cost centre into a moat-funding engine. Every kitten that enters through a clinic arrives at near-zero CAC and with the feeding engine already informed. That fixes the two biggest model sensitivities: acquisition volume and CAC.
  • It sets up the one convention effect available in this market. Once breeders and shelters issue verified histories, buyers start asking for them, and supply follows demand. That is how a document becomes a standard.
  • It is legitimate dominance. It is useful to vets, portable for owners and non-exclusive, so it survives GAC scrutiny, PDPL and a public story about "a company that owns cats' data."
  • It's what makes Moracat worth hundreds of millions. A cat-food subscription gets priced like a retailer. A registry that clinics write to and the market relies on at every hand-over gets priced as infrastructure, and is the asset Mars, Nestlé, a Gulf consolidator or the state itself would need to buy rather than build.

What it costs you: saying no to Jeddah until Riyadh districts are dense, and no to a broad store, a native app or a GCC launch. Hire a clinic partnerships lead before a second marketer. Put CVAC ahead of GMV on every board slide.
