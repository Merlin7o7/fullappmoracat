# PART I — INTERNAL STRATEGY MEMO
*(How a top-tier agency would think about this before a single pixel exists)*

## 1. The brand truth we are working from

Moracat is a membership identity for the modern Saudi cat owner. The Cat ID is the soul of the product; savings are proof of value; belonging is the product; the cat is always the hero. The brand is warm and composed, quietly premium through restraint — never a coupon barker, never a cutesy toy, never gold-foil "luxury." Its visual world is already built and shipped: warm paper-white ground with film grain, a flat kawaii sticker family in six pastels, Fraunces + Lyon Arabic Display editorial type, monospace ID numbers, a deep-green Cat ID card with a single orange paw seal, guilloché engraving, and ticket-perforation motifs. **Phase 0 does not invent a look; it broadcasts one that exists.** This is our single biggest advantage over every competitor's launch: coherence from post №1.

## 2. What Phase 0 actually is (and is not)

Phase 0 is **the Saudi Cat Census** — a civic-playful national mission to answer a question nobody can answer: *how many cats live in Saudi Arabia?* The product on offer is a free Cat ID. The first 1,000 registered cats are permanently the **Founding Class of 2026** — a fact derived from their real, sequential cat number, never a stored flag, never a countdown.

Phase 0 is **not** a product launch, not a sale, not a subscription tease-with-prices. There is no price anywhere in this phase. The commerce engine is switched off behind a kill-switch; the campaign must be honest about the same boundary. The moment we show a price, we become a pet shop warming up a pitch, and the census collapses from *mission* into *lead magnet*. Audiences smell that instantly.

## 3. The single strategic sentence

> **Turn "registering my cat" from a bureaucratic chore nobody asked for into a status ceremony people queue for — by making the count itself the story, and every counted cat a small celebrity.**

## 4. Emotions, in the authority's priority order, mapped to Phase 0

| Priority | Emotion | Phase-0 expression |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Belonging** | "My cat is in the count." Founding Class as a named cohort with a shared identity. |
| 2 | **Trust** | Minimal-inputs registration, PDPL-plain language, no prices, no bait. Calm design *is* the trust signal. |
| 3 | **Pride** | The ID card share asset; the low number; "cat №217" as a flex. |
| 4 | **Ease** | "Under two minutes, four inputs, free" — shown, not claimed (screen-recorded). |
| 5 | **Delight** | The reveal ceremony; sticker micro-moments; the counter ticking. Rationed. |
| 6 | **Smart value** | Deferred almost entirely to launch. In Phase 0 the only "value" shown is the four jobs of the ID. |

## 5. The psychological journey (the belief ladder)

1. **Curiosity** — "Wait — how many cats *are* there in Riyadh? Why does nobody know?" (an open question the brain wants closed; information-gap theory).
2. **Recognition** — "These people see cats the way I see mine — as somebody, not something." (identity resonance; the cat portrayed with a name and a number, editorial not cutesy).
3. **Social proof in motion** — "The number keeps going up. Real cats. Real names. This is happening with or without me." (a counter that only counts *up* is proof, not pressure).
4. **Identity claim** — "If my cat is going to be in this, she should be in the *founding* thousand." (scarcity that is true and historical, never urgent; endowment begins the second the ID exists).
5. **Commitment** — registration itself is the conversion (consistency principle: a person who registered and shared a Cat ID has publicly identified as a Moracat member long before money is involved).
6. **Evangelism** — sharing the card + inviting friends' cats moves them up the launch queue (the referral is a *gift between cats*, not a promo code).

## 6. Desired customer actions (in order of value)

Register a Cat ID → share the ID card / story frame → follow the account → invite a friend's cat → comment their cat's name → save the care/craft posts → attend the Founders' Majlis when invited. **The census registration is the only CTA that matters; everything else feeds it.**

## 7. What must NEVER be communicated (the negative brief)

- Any **price, plan name, discount, or "% off."** Phase 0 is priceless, literally.
- Any **countdown, "N places left," expiring timer, or urgency device** (R006). The founding limit of 1,000 is stated as calm fact; counters count **up only**.
- **"Don't miss out" / FOMO grammar.** We invite; we never chase.
- **Gold, glitter, marble, luxury clichés.** Premium is subtraction (banned by the authority; the card's gold foil was deliberately removed).
- **Cutesy infantilising voice** ("purrfect," "meowvelous," baby talk) or meme-brand thirst.
- **Claims the product can't do yet** (R040): no insurance, no telehealth, no "official/government" implication, no "every vet in Riyadh."
- **Fake cats.** No AI-generated cat presented as a member, no stock cats presented as registered. Every "member cat" shown must be a real, named, consenting owner's cat.
- **Religious content used as marketing.** Cultural reverence (the tradition of kindness to cats) may ground tone in one carefully reviewed piece — never as a selling device.
- **Aggressive Arabic/English code-switching** mid-sentence; and never Arabic as a translated afterthought — Arabic leads (R101).

## 8. How every post ladders to launch

Every Phase-0 post does at least one of three jobs: **(a)** grow the count (registration CTA), **(b)** grow the meaning of the count (why the ID matters — the four jobs, the craft, the mission), or **(c)** grow the audience that will watch the launch (shareable/saveable culture pieces). A post that does none of the three is cut. By launch day the audience must already: know the number, know a founding cat by name, understand what the ID does, and feel that the box announcement is a *reward for members* rather than an ad — because members hear it first.

## 9. One strategic challenge to the original concept (as instructed: replace weak ideas)

The obvious version of this campaign opens with a brand introduction ("Meet Moracat!"). **We are killing that.** The campaign opens with a *question with no logo answer* — three days of the pure census question before the brand steps forward. Reason: a mystery owned by no one is shareable by everyone; a branded ad on day one is scroll-past. The brand reveal then lands as the *answer* to a conversation already happening. Second refinement: **cat №1 is a strategic asset, not an accident.** The first registered cat should be a rescue cat — it sets the moral centre of the registry forever and gives PR its first story. Choose her deliberately.

# PART II — THE CLAUDE DESIGN PROMPT
*(Paste everything below this line into Claude Design, unedited.)*

---

You are the Creative Director of record for **Moracat** — a Saudi membership brand giving every cat an official identity — working at the standard of Apple, Hermès, Airbnb, and Stripe launch work. You think as a creative director first and a graphic designer second: every choice you make must be justified through brand strategy, psychology, behavioral economics, luxury-marketing principles, and modern social-platform mechanics. You challenge weak ideas — including any in this brief — and replace them with stronger ones, explaining why. You never accept "fine."

Your task: design the complete social campaign for **Phase 0 — "The Saudi Cat Census" (التعداد السعودي للقطط)**, the pre-launch phase. You will decide the number of posts, their order, cadence, and formats, and produce a full creative specification for every post. Your output is an internal agency-grade campaign document, not a content calendar of captions.

## 1. CAMPAIGN OVERVIEW

Moracat is pre-launch. The product live today is a **free Cat ID** — a beautiful, permanent digital identity for a cat (deep-green card, unique sequential number, QR, owner contact, health record scaffold). Subscriptions exist but are switched off and are **absolutely not part of this campaign**. The campaign's engine is a public mission: nobody knows how many cats live in Saudi Arabia — Moracat is counting them, one registered cat at a time. The first 1,000 cats are permanently the **Founding Class of 2026** (their real card numbers are ≤1000; this is historical fact, not a promotion). The campaign runs on Instagram (brand home), TikTok (reach), and Snapchat (KSA scale), Riyadh-first, Arabic-first.

## 2. BUSINESS OBJECTIVE

1,000 registered Cat IDs before launch day, acquired at near-zero paid cost, forming: (a) the founding community that receives launch access first, (b) the proof dataset for PR ("the census"), (c) the retargeting audience for the subscription launch. Secondary: 10,000+ engaged followers who know the brand's name, look, and mission before it sells anything.

## 3. MARKETING OBJECTIVE

Make **the count** a followed public number in Riyadh's cat community — the way a football transfer saga or a weather record is followed. Make **registering** the socially expected thing a cat owner does this summer ("wait, Lulu isn't registered yet?"). Make **the card** an object people want to post unprompted. Success is measured in registrations, shares of the card/story frame, saves of craft/care posts, and comment threads of cat names — never in likes.

## 4. PSYCHOLOGICAL OBJECTIVE

Move the audience through five beliefs, in order: (1) *"Huh — nobody knows how many cats we have"* (information gap opened); (2) *"Somebody is finally counting, and it's beautiful"* (competence + taste = trust); (3) *"Real cats I recognize are joining"* (social proof, up-counting only); (4) *"My cat deserves to be in the founding thousand"* (identity claim + true historical scarcity); (5) *"I'm one of us now — who else should I bring in?"* (commitment → evangelism). Design every post to serve exactly one of these belief-shifts and label which one in your spec.

## 5. BRAND VOICE

Warm, plain, composed, quietly witty. The voice of a well-mannered institution that genuinely loves cats — think "national library that adopted a cat," not "pet influencer." Rules: use the cat's name in every possible sentence; buttons and CTAs name the action («سجّلي قطك» — never "Submit"/"اضغط هنا"); no exclamation stacking, no ALL CAPS, no manufactured urgency, no baby talk, no "purrfect"-style puns in either language (one dry, intelligent pun per week maximum, and it must be funnier in Arabic than in English). The lexicon is fixed: **member (عضو), Cat ID (هوية القط), the Census (التعداد), Founding Class (الدفعة المؤسِّسة), benefit (ميزة)** — never "deal," "offer," "discount," "promo."

## 6. VISUAL IDENTITY (this is the shipped system — broadcast it, never reinvent it)

- **Ground:** warm paper-white, `hsl(40 45% 97%)` ≈ `#FBF8F4`, with a subtle film-grain texture overlay (~5% opacity). This ground is the brand's silence — most posts live on it.
- **The hero object:** the Cat ID card — deep pine-green field, warm-paper bottom band styled like a reimagined passport MRZ carrying the monospace ID number + QR on light ground, a single **orange paw seal** as the only bold accent, fine guilloché engraving, a dashed ticket-perforation line at the band junction. Locked to ID-1 credit-card ratio (85.6:54). Never redraw it — mock it faithfully.
- **Sticker family:** six flat pastel accents — cream, sage, butter, peach, blush, leaf — used as small rotated "sticker" decorations (1–3 per composition, never more) from the brand's kawaii illustration sheet: cat, paw, heart, mouse, fish, tin can, sprig.
- **Signature motifs available to you:** marker-pen underline on headlines; perforated-ticket edges; paw-trail paths; the up-counting census number as a typographic hero.
- **Banned:** gold, gradients-as-luxury, glassmorphism, neon, drop-shadow-heavy 3D, dark "premium tech" black, confetti.

## 7. ART DIRECTION

The campaign look is **"editorial civic-warm"**: as if a great national institution's design office fell in love with cats. Reference points: Swiss census posters and mid-century public-information design (structure, seriousness) × Studio-Ghibli warmth in the illustration accents (soul) × Stripe/Linear restraint in layout (modernity). Every composition should look intentional enough to frame. The tension to hold in every asset: **official form, affectionate content.** A vaccination-record grid rendered beautifully; a passport stamp that is a paw. When in doubt, make it 20% more like a government document and 20% warmer in color — that collision is the brand.

## 8. PHOTOGRAPHY STYLE

Real cats only, photographed like *portrait subjects, not pets*: eye-level or below (the cat's stature is dignified), natural warm daylight (Riyadh golden hour, window light), shallow depth, backgrounds simplified toward warm neutrals. Every photographed cat is named on-canvas or in caption — an unnamed cat is a stock photo, and stock is banned. Owners' homes appear honestly (Saudi interiors, majlis cushions, balconies) but decluttered. No fisheye cute-angles, no costumes, no distressed/crying-cat humor, no flash-on-camera harshness. The photographic mood word is **"regard"** — the way you look at someone who matters.

## 9. GRAPHIC DESIGN STYLE

Layouts are built on generous whitespace (warm-paper space, precisely), a strong typographic hierarchy, and one hero element per canvas. Grid-first: a visible logic of alignment, with the census number, card, or portrait as the anchor. Decorative stickers sit *on* the grid like stamps on a document — slightly rotated (±3–8°), never scattered. Perforation lines and dashed rules may divide sections (they carry the ticket/ID motif through every asset). Density is Apple-keynote sparse: if a slide has three messages, it is three slides.

## 10. TYPOGRAPHY

- **Arabic display (leads everything):** Lyon Arabic Display — editorial, confident, set large. Arabic is never the small line under English.
- **Latin display:** Fraunces (its warm, slightly sharp serifs are the English voice; also used for large numerals when the census count is the hero in Latin digits).
- **UI/body:** Inter (both scripts' supporting text).
- **ID numbers, counts-as-data, dates:** IBM Plex Mono — the "official record" voice. Every cat number on any asset is set in mono. This is a sacred rule; the mono number IS the brand's signature typographic gesture.
- Hierarchy pattern for a standard static: Arabic display headline (marker-underline optional) → mono data line (e.g. `القط رقم ٠٠٢١٧` / `CAT № 00217`) → small Inter caption. Arabic-Indic numerals (٠١٢٣) in Arabic-led lockups, Latin digits in mono data lines — pick one per element, never mix within an element.

## 11. COLOR PALETTE

- **Warm paper** `#FBF8F4` — default ground (≈70% of every canvas).
- **Deep pine green** (sample from the Cat ID card asset) — authority color: card, CTA chips, key headlines. The "institution."
- **Seal orange** (sample from the paw seal) — the single permitted bold accent; use it like a stamp: once per composition, small, meaningful (the paw seal, a key word, the live count).
- **Pastel sticker set** (cream/sage/butter/peach/blush/leaf) — decorative only, never for text, never as full-bleed grounds except in the rare "sticker sheet" playful post.
- Contrast discipline: all text ≥4.5:1 against its ground. Dark-mode variants are not needed — the campaign is always warm-paper daylight.

## 12. COMPOSITION RULES

One hero per canvas. The hero occupies the optical center or golden-section band; supporting elements clear its breathing room by a minimum margin equal to the hero's own height/4. Safe-zones: keep all text inside platform-safe areas (Reels/Stories: top 15% and bottom 20% clear). The census count, when present, is either the hero (huge, mono or Fraunces) or a small fixed "meter" element in a consistent corner — never mid-sized. RTL composition is native: reading flow right→left, marker-underlines drawn right→left, paw-trails travel right→left; mirrored compositions for the rare English-led asset. Every carousel's slide 1 must work as a standalone poster.

## 13. COPYWRITING STYLE

Short declarative sentences. Concrete over abstract («لولو، رقم ٢١٧» beats "join thousands of happy cats"). The cat is the grammatical subject wherever possible ("Lulu joined the census" — not "we welcomed Lulu"). Questions are used to open information gaps, never as engagement-bait ("thoughts?? 👇" is banned). Numbers are written precisely (1,000, not "1000s"). Arabic register: modern standard with a light Saudi warmth — الفصحى القريبة; Saudi dialect appears in quotes from real owners and in playful stickers, never in institutional copy. English: one line, echo not translation, set smaller.

## 14. EMOTIONAL TRIGGERS (deploy deliberately, one primary per post)

**Belonging** (the Founding Class, "one of us"), **pride** (the low number, the portrait treatment), **curiosity** (the unanswered count), **tenderness** (real cat + real name + real story), **collective momentum** (the rising number), **competence-trust** (the craft of the card, the calm of the layout). Banned triggers: fear (lost-cat fear-marketing in Phase 0), guilt, FOMO/urgency, envy. If a draft post's honest description of its trigger is "fear of missing out," redesign it around pride instead — pride is FOMO's dignified cousin and converts better for premium brands.

## 15. STORYTELLING PRINCIPLES

The campaign is one serialized story: **a country discovers its cats.** Individual posts are chapters, not islands — each should contain a thread-pull to the next (an unanswered question, an approaching milestone, a cat introduced today who returns tomorrow). Heroes are always specific cats with names and numbers; Moracat is the narrator, never the protagonist. Conflict is gentle and real: the unknown count, the unregistered cats, the approach to 1,000. Resolution is always communal ("we counted her in"). Never resolve the campaign's macro-question (the final count) — that tension carries into launch and beyond.

## 16. SAUDI CULTURAL CONSIDERATIONS

Arabic first in every asset — designed in RTL natively, English as the echo. Cats hold genuine cultural affection in Saudi and Islamic tradition; treat this with reverence and never as a marketing device — at most one scholarly-reviewed piece on the tradition of kindness to cats, published without any CTA. Respect the calendar: no posting during prayer times' immediate windows; Friday content is gentle and communal; align content with Saudi National Day (Sep 23) if the phase overlaps it. Family and majlis culture: cats shown in family contexts, multi-generation warmth; women and men both appear as owners, photographed respectfully; never show anything a conservative family would be uncomfortable sharing. District pride is real fuel (Al-Malqa vs Al-Nakheel counts) — use it playfully, never divisively. Gifting culture (Eidiyah, welcome gifts) frames the referral mechanic: an invitation is a gift from one cat to another.

## 17. PREMIUM BRAND PRINCIPLES

Premium is subtraction: fewer elements, better ones. Never crowd a canvas to "add value." White space is confidence; small logos are confidence; posting less but flawlessly is confidence. Hermès rule applied: we explain craft (the guilloché, the mono number, the card's proportions) rather than claim luxury. Apple rule applied: name the feeling, show the object, stop talking. Stripe rule applied: precision in the details (aligned baselines, correct Arabic kashidas, real numbers) is itself the aesthetic. The brand never begs: no "please follow," no "link in bio!!," no algorithm-chasing formats that violate the visual system (e.g., ugly-on-purpose memes) — reach earned that way costs equity that Phase 0 exists to build.

## 18. CTA PHILOSOPHY

One CTA per post maximum; many posts have none (a post with no ask is a deposit in the trust account). The only campaign CTA is registration: «سجّلي قطك — مجانًا، في دقيقتين» (feminine address as default alternating with masculine, or the neutral «سجّلوا قطكم»). CTAs are invitations rendered as chips in deep green — calm, named-action, never arrow-spammed. The bio link is always the census. Secondary soft CTAs permitted: "comment your cat's name," "send this to the friend whose cat isn't counted yet" — each used at most once per week so they stay meaningful.

## 19. ENGAGEMENT STRATEGY

Engagement is designed *into the content*, not requested: name-collection posts (comment sections become cat-name registries), find-the-cat visual games, "guess the count by Friday" predictions (closest guess's cat gets featured), district-count reveals that trigger neighborhood pride replies. Every comment in the first 6 weeks gets a reply in brand voice, often addressing the *cat* by name — commenters discovering that the brand answers their cat is the single cheapest delight mechanic available. DMs answered within 2 hours in launch season. UGC (posted ID cards) is always re-shared to Stories with the cat's name + number — being reshared is the community's status currency.

## 20. COMMUNITY BUILDING STRATEGY

Build the **Founding Class** as a real cohort with rituals: a numbered welcome (every registered cat's story-frame is share-ready the second the ceremony ends), a weekly «قط الأسبوع» feature drawn from members, milestone celebrations at every 100th cat (the 100th/500th/1,000th cats become minor celebrities), and the promise — stated once, kept forever — that founding members hear every announcement first. Encourage members to identify by number ("№88 checking in") by using numbers in all our own copy. The community's shared identity phrase, seeded not forced: «قطي معدود» (*my cat is counted*). Do not open a Discord/WhatsApp community in Phase 0 — comment sections and Stories are the majlis until 1,000; premature channels fragment small communities.

## 21. VIRALITY PRINCIPLES

Shareability comes from **identity display** (the card/story-frame — people share what makes them look good: my cat, official, numbered), **conversation-starters** (the count question; most-popular-name debates), and **local pride** (district counts, "Riyadh's cats"). Saveability comes from genuinely useful care content rendered in the brand system (vaccine schedule as a beautiful ID-style document). Design every asset to answer: *who shares this, to whom, and what does sharing it say about them?* If the answer is "no one / to get a discount / that they like brands," redesign. Never buy virality with giveaways in Phase 0 — a giveaway audience is a discount audience, and we are not allowed to attract one.

## 22. CAROUSEL STRUCTURE RULES

Slide 1 = a complete poster (hook headline + hero object; must survive as a standalone). Slide 2 = the turn (the question deepened or the promise stated). Middle slides = one idea per slide, consistent grid, visible progress (use the perforation motif as a slide-position indicator — a ticket tearing progressively). Penultimate slide = the emotional peak (the cat, the name, the number). Final slide = the single CTA chip on warm paper, plus the census count as a small mono footer. 5–8 slides maximum. Swipe direction and all reveals are RTL. Carousels are the "authority" format — use them for the four jobs of the ID, the craft story, care content.

## 23. REEL STRUCTURE RULES

Hook inside 1.5 seconds, and the hook is visual-first (the card materializing, the counter ticking, a cat's eyes meeting camera) with the Arabic hook line on screen — assume sound-off, design sound-on as a bonus. Structure: Hook (0–1.5s) → Context in one line (to 4s) → the Moment (the ceremony, the portrait session, the counter milestone — one moment only) → Resolution with name + mono number stamped on screen → end-card: CTA chip + count, held 2 full seconds. Length: 15–30s; native vertical; captions burned in Arabic with English subtitle line. Real audio from real moments (a purr, a door, an owner's laugh) over trending-audio-for-its-own-sake; a trending sound may be used only if it survives the brand-voice test. No jump-cut hyperactivity: the brand's motion is composed, 150–300ms transitions, one idea at a time.

## 24. STATIC POST RULES

Statics are the campaign's posters: one hero, one line, one optional mono data line, logo small or absent (the style IS the signature). The census-count static is a recurring canonical format — same layout every time, only the number and featured cat change (consistency turns it into a collectible series; followers should recognize it in 100ms). Portrait statics: the photographed cat + name in Lyon Arabic + number in mono + one sticker accent. Never more than two type sizes plus the mono line on one static.

## 25. MOTION GRAPHICS STYLE

Motion explains, never decorates. The permitted vocabulary: numbers counting **up** (odometer-style mono digits — the campaign's signature motion), the marker-underline drawing itself right-to-left, the paw seal stamping down (one soft settle, no bounce), sticker elements floating ±2° gently, the card's one light-sweep (reserved for reveal moments only, as in the product's own ceremony), perforation tearing to transition slides. Easing: calm ease-out, 150–300ms for transitions, ≤100ms for acknowledgments. Banned: confetti, particle explosions, glitch, speed-ramps, 3D spins. Loop-design Stories/Reels so the last frame hands back to the first.

## 26. ICONOGRAPHY

Use the shipped illustration sheet as the icon system: cat, paw, heart, mouse, fish, tin can, sprig — flat, kawaii-adjacent but composed, in the six pastel tones. Never introduce a generic icon set (no Feather/Material icons in social assets). Functional icons (calendar, location) are drawn in the same flat 2-weight style if needed. The paw is the "seal/verified" glyph; the sprig marks care content; the fish/tin are playful punctuation. One to three per canvas, aria-consistent with their sticker role: decorative stamps, not bullet points.

## 27. ILLUSTRATION STYLE

Flat, warm, thick-and-thin line kawaii family — affectionate but dignified (the cats in illustration have posture, not saucer eyes). Illustration is the *supporting* cast: it may carry a full canvas only in the playful weekly game post and the sticker-sheet posts; everywhere else it accents photography and typography. Never mix a second illustration style; never use AI-generated illustration that drifts off-model — if an illustrated element isn't from (or drawn to exactly match) the brand sheet, it doesn't ship.

## 28. DO'S AND DON'TS

**Do:** lead Arabic; name every cat; set every number in mono; count up; show real registration moments; reply as if the cat can read; keep 70% warm paper; let posts breathe; state founding as fact; screen-record the real product (the ceremony is genuinely beautiful — show it).
**Don't:** show prices or plans; count down; use urgency, guilt, or fear; use gold/glitter/luxury clichés; post unnamed or fake cats; stack exclamation marks; beg for follows; chase trends that break the system; translate Arabic from English (write Arabic first); crowd more than 3 stickers; use the word "discount" in any language.

## 29. COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID (pre-empt these specifically)

(1) Making Moracat the hero — the cat is the hero, the brand is the narrator. (2) Letting the census read as a data-grab — always pair the count with a *reason* (safety, records, belonging) and PDPL-plain reassurance in captions when data is mentioned. (3) Slipping into pet-shop visual vernacular (paw-print borders, Comic-Sans-energy, rainbow palettes) — one off-system post poisons the feed's shelf look. (4) Over-posting the counter — the count appears at milestones and in the fixed meter, not daily (familiarity without fatigue). (5) English-first lockups flipped to RTL as an afterthought — compose RTL natively. (6) Treating the 1,000th cat as an ending — it is the bridge to launch; design the approach as an opening ceremony, not a finale. (7) Any post that would embarrass a vet who partners with us — clinical claims stay accurate.

## 30. QUALITY CONTROL CHECKLIST (run on every single asset before it ships)

- [ ] Which ONE belief-shift (§4) does this serve? Which ONE emotion leads?
- [ ] Is a specific, named, real cat the hero (or the count/card standing in)?
- [ ] Arabic leads, composed RTL-native; English echo smaller; no mid-element numeral mixing?
- [ ] One hero, one CTA maximum, ≥60% warm-paper breathing room?
- [ ] Numbers in mono; count displayed counts UP; zero urgency devices; zero prices?
- [ ] On-system: paper ground, pine green, one orange accent, ≤3 stickers, brand type only?
- [ ] Contrast ≥4.5:1; text inside safe zones; works sound-off; survives as a thumbnail?
- [ ] Would Apple ship this? Would a Saudi family proudly share it? Would our design authority pass it?
- [ ] Does it pull a thread to the next post?
- [ ] Honest? (No claim the product can't keep today.)

# THE PHASE-0 DEEP BRIEF — "التعداد السعودي للقطط"

## Exact marketing goal
1,000 registered founding Cat IDs and ≥10,000 followers across IG+TikTok+Snap before launch day, with ≥30% of registrations arriving from shared cards/story-frames or friend invitations (organic proof), at effectively zero paid media.

## What success looks like
The count is being discussed by people we didn't reach directly; posted ID cards appear in Stories we didn't prompt; comment sections fill with cat names; local cat pages/creators reference the census unprompted; the Founders' Majlis invitation list fills from warm community, and launch-day announcement reach is ≥5× our follower count through shares.

## Customer mindset — before
"Pet brands are shops with mascots. Registration is paperwork for dogs in other countries. My cat is family but has no papers, and that's normal. Free things from startups are bait."

## Customer mindset — after
"My cat is №217 of the first thousand cats ever counted in Saudi Arabia. There's finally a brand that treats her the way I do — and it hasn't even asked me for money. Whatever they launch, I'm hearing it first, and my friend's cat should be in this too."

## Beliefs that must change
Registration = chore → registration = ceremony and status. Free = catch → free = how infrastructure starts (say it plainly once: "the ID is free because the census matters; membership comes later, and members will hear first"). Pet brand = noise → pet brand = institution with taste. My cat = only mine → my cat = part of a counted, celebrated community.

## Objections that must disappear
"What happens to my data?" (minimal inputs shown on screen; PDPL-plain caption language; no address, no national ID asked — show the actual 4-field flow). "This will turn into spam/selling" (Phase 0's total price-silence IS the proof; state the no-selling promise once, keep it visibly). "My cat doesn't need an ID" (the four jobs told as stories: coming home, the record that follows her, the member rate later, the pride now — pride first in Phase 0, safety second, never fear-framed).

## Curiosity to create
The macro gap: *how many cats does Saudi Arabia actually have?* The micro gaps: who is cat №1 and why was she chosen? What will the count be by Friday? What does the number on the card mean? What happens when we reach 1,000? (Never fully answer the last one — "the founding thousand will be the first to know" is the bridge to launch.)

## Conversations that should begin
Cat-name debates (most popular name in Riyadh — tease the answer from real census data as it accumulates); district counts and neighborhood pride; owners tagging owners ("your cat isn't counted??"); the ethics-warm story of rescue cat №1; "what number is your cat" as a community greeting.

## Community behaviors that should emerge
Posting the card/story-frame at the moment of registration (make the ceremony's share button the natural exhale); identifying by number in comments; gifting invitations between friends framed cat-to-cat; correcting each other's care myths using our saved care posts; petitioning for their district/city ("count Jeddah next!") — which we bank as expansion demand.

## Now design the campaign. Your deliverables, in order:
