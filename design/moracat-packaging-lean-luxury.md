# MRC-PKG-002 — Lean Luxury Revision (Rev B)

> **Objective:** maximum perceived value per riyal. This revision challenges every cost
> assumption in MRC-PKG-001 (Rev A) and rebuilds the system around one thesis:
> **everything the member *feels* as luxury is pacing, precision, ink and words —
> none of which scale with material cost.** Where Rev A bought an effect, Rev B
> engineers it.
>
> Supersedes the cost sections (§03 tray spec, §06 finishes, §10 sourcing, §11 tables)
> of `moracat-packaging-dossier.md/.html`. The experience goals, cat-safety spec,
> opening choreography and design language of Rev A remain in force. The design
> authority (`design/DESIGN-AUTHORITY.md`) wins every conflict. 2026-07 · SAR.

The member-visible experience is *not* reduced — three of the changes below arguably
**improve** it. What was cut is material the member never consciously registered:
a second tissue sheet, a duplicated tray board, four-colour process on a card that
reads better in one colour, and imported rigidity whose job deboss + weight of stock
does locally.

## 1 · The five principles that replace spend

These are the strategic levers. Every line-item change in §2 is one of these applied.

1. **Ink is the cheapest luxury there is.** A flexo plate is paid for once; after that,
   every printed word, frame, pattern and Arabic line is effectively free. Rev B moves
   as much of the experience as possible *onto surfaces we already print*: the lid
   message, the "open gently" phrase, the card frame, the den door graphics. Hermès'
   equity is one colour used with total conviction — that costs plates, not per-box.
2. **One blank does two jobs.** Every separate die-cut piece carries its own material,
   die, setup, and pick-line touch. Merging pieces (divider + stage tray → one blank)
   saves twice: board *and* labour.
3. **Precision is perceived engineering.** A pouch that clicks into a die-cut comb with
   zero slack *feels* like Apple regardless of board grade. Tolerance costs die-maker
   skill once; sloppiness costs brand forever. Spend on the die, never on the board.
4. **Scarcity outperforms frequency.** One foil number on one keepsake per member's
   lifetime beats foil monthly — emotionally *and* financially. Rev B concentrates
   rigidity and foil into fewer, rarer, more meaningful objects (annual, not quarterly).
5. **Words are the finish.** "For Louz — August's provisions" on 300gsm warm stock in
   one deep green does more than 4-colour process ever did. The authority already says
   words are interface (R081–R087); Rev B makes words the *material*.

## 2 · The changes, one by one

Format per recommendation: **why cheaper · why still (or more) premium · saving at
5k/mo · trade-offs**.

### 2.1 Merge the stage tray into the divider — "the Curtain" ★ biggest structural win

Rev A shipped a B-flute cellar divider (1.00) **plus** a separate E-flute stage tray
(1.90). Rev B replaces both with **one nested die-cut blank**: the divider's walls
extend into a fold-over top panel — *the curtain* — whose upper face is printed 1C
green (the welcome line, a frame for the card, thumb-notch marked). Open the lid →
printed curtain with the card resting in its frame → lift at the notch → provisions.

- **Why cheaper:** one blank, one die, one feed through the flexo line, one pick-line
  touch instead of two; nesting the curtain into the divider's die layout consumes
  board that was previously trim waste. 2.90 → 1.45 (T2–T4); 1.80 → 1.30 (T1).
- **Why still premium:** the member's reveal sequence is *identical* — lid, printed
  veil, card, lift, pantry. A printed panel with a clean die-cut notch reads as more
  considered than a loose tray; nothing rattles, nothing lifts out crooked. The cotton
  lift-loops are replaced by two die-cut thumb notches — Muji's honesty, zero cost.
- **Saving:** −1.45/box (T2–T4), −0.35 (T1). Also removes the cotton loops (−0.10 in
  the tray's cost) and ~20 s of assembly.
- **Trade-offs:** the curtain doesn't lift out as a "tray of gifts" — discovery items
  now sit *in* die-cut wells of the curtain panel. V1 must confirm the wells present as
  a stage, not as slots. If the film review disagrees, T3/T4 alone reinstate a tray
  (+1.9 only where it earns).

### 2.2 Print the phrases, not the tape

Rev A specified custom-printed water-activated tape (0.38). Rev B uses **plain
reinforced kraft WAT** and moves the brand's tape moment onto the box itself: the
thread-line and **افتح بهدوء — أحدهم يراقب** ("open gently — someone is watching")
flexo-printed *alongside the tape path* — already on the plate, zero marginal cost.

- **Why cheaper:** stock tape at commodity price; no custom tape MOQ (custom WAT runs
  ~50k-metre minimums that tie cash at our stage). 0.38 → 0.22.
- **Why still premium:** the joke lands in exactly the same second — printed guide
  line, phrase, knife pass. Nobody keeps tape; everybody reads the box.
- **Saving:** −0.16/box, plus ~8,000 SAR of custom-tape MOQ cash freed.
- **Trade-offs:** tape itself is unbranded (plain kraft). Mitigation: the Ø60 mm seal
  sticker (kept — 0.12, the highest delight-per-riyal item in the system) sits at the
  tape's terminus, so the closure still signs off with the brand.

### 2.3 One tissue veil, not a double wrap

Rev A dressed the stage with two printed tissue sheets (0.55). Rev B uses **one plain
cream half-sheet** laid as a veil over the curtain's card frame only.

- **Why cheaper:** half the paper, and plain stock (Paperpros/Napco shelf item) beats a
  custom-printed tissue run. 0.55 → 0.20.
- **Why still premium:** tissue's entire job is a two-second parting gesture; one veil
  performs it fully. Aesop wraps in *less* material than mass brands — restraint reads
  as confidence. The printed motif moves to the sticker (already printed).
- **Saving:** −0.35/box.
- **Trade-offs:** less "fluff" in photos of the open box. Counter: the curtain's
  printed face replaces the visual softness with graphic intent — and cats prefer the
  tissue on the floor anyway, where it becomes den bedding (kept, deliberately).

### 2.4 The monthly card: one colour, heavier hand, name added in-house

Rev A: A5 350gsm, 4/4 digital variable-data (0.65). Rev B: **A6 folded, 300gsm warm
uncoated, 1-colour deep green offset shells gang-printed quarterly in Jeddah**; the
cat's name and month over-printed at the fulfilment line (small mono overprinter —
the same class of machine that already prints shipping labels).

- **Why cheaper:** gang-run offset shells at quarterly volume cost a fraction of
  monthly 4-colour digital VDP; in-house overprint costs ~0.02/card in toner.
  0.65 → 0.28.
- **Why still premium:** this is the Monocle move — one colour on thick warm stock
  reads *more* editorial than four-colour digital, which always carries a faint
  home-printer association. The name is still there, in the member's hands, every
  month (R001, R082). Deboss-look masthead achieved with a tint plate.
- **Saving:** −0.37/box — the whole system's second-biggest recurring save.
- **Trade-offs:** no monthly full-colour photography on the card. Counter: the app owns
  photography; the card owns words. Seasonal colour variety comes from rotating the
  *ink* of the sticker sheet, not the card press run.

### 2.5 The Welcome Folio goes local and folded — "the Passport"

Rev A imported a 2 mm rigid wrapped folio (11.00 landed). Rev B: **an 800gsm duplex
board folio, die-cut and folded locally** — same passport-wallet gesture, die-cut
cradle slots for the Cat ID card and collar tag, blind deboss seal, and the **brass
foil reserved solely for the cat's ID number** applied by MMC Print n Gift or Arabian
Print Pack in Jeddah.

- **Why cheaper:** no rigid set-up labour, no wrap paper, no import freight/QC/buffer
  stock, no moulded-pulp cradle tooling (the folio nests in a die-cut well of the
  curtain in box one). 11.00 → 4.50.
- **Why still premium:** 800gsm has genuine hand (it's the weight of a hardback cover);
  the deboss + single foil number carry the ceremony; the *content* — the cat's name,
  the number, the tag — is the luxury (R031/R032). Tiffany's power is protocol, not
  board thickness: the folio is still only ever issued with a Cat ID, never empty.
- **Saving:** −6.50 per new member (−59%). At 500 new members/mo: ~39,000 SAR/yr.
- **Trade-offs:** less rigid heft in the hand than a set-up box. Mitigation: die-cut a
  finger-pull and make the fold *snap* (double-crease) — mechanical satisfaction
  substitutes for mass. If churn cohorts show the folio matters more than modelled,
  upgrading it back is a one-line change that touches nothing else.

### 2.6 The Den Box: annual, local, and wrapped quarterly — scarcity as strategy

Rev A imported a rigid drawer box quarterly for Tier 4 (3.60/mo amortised). Rev B:
**quarterly gifts arrive furoshiki-style** — wrapped in a printed paper wrap closed
with cotton tape and a seal sticker, sitting in the curtain's centre well — and the
rigid drawer becomes **annual**: one locally litho-laminated E-flute drawer
("سنة مع لوز" — *a year with Louz*) marking the membership anniversary, produced by
UCIC litho-lam with MMC finishing.

- **Why cheaper:** wrap + tape + sticker ≈ 0.90/quarter; one local drawer ≈ 5.50/yr.
  Monthly amortisation 3.60 → 0.90. No import lane at all.
- **Why still premium:** *scarcity outperforms frequency.* Four identical rigid boxes a
  year become furniture-drawer clutter; one annual keepsake tied to the anniversary
  (R065's value recap made physical) is an event. The quarterly wrap is the Japanese
  lesson done properly — the unwrapping is the gift. And the anniversary drawer
  arrives with the year's savings total printed inside the lid: the anti-churn number
  (R041) as an object.
- **Saving:** −2.70/mo per Tier 4 member (−75% of keepsake budget).
- **Trade-offs:** Tier 4 members receive less rigid *stuff*. Watch quarterly NPS; if
  the wrap underperforms, the fallback is a semi-annual drawer (still −50%).

### 2.7 One shipper die instead of two

Rev A specified M-3 (500×360×220) for Tier 4. Rev B **retires M-3**: Tier 4 ships in
M-2 with contents sized to fit (the accessory/toy spec already fits; Den-annual months
use a second small parcel only if genuinely needed).

- **Why cheaper:** one die, one plate, one board spec, one warehouse SKU; pooled volume
  pushes M-2 down the price curve (5.60 → 5.20 at 5k). Tier 4 shipper cost drops from
  ~6.50 to 5.20.
- **Why still premium:** members compare their box month-to-month, not tier-to-tier. A
  denser, perfectly packed M-2 out-performs a roomier M-3 with more void.
- **Saving:** −0.40 every box, −1.30 on Tier 4 boxes.
- **Trade-offs:** a hard ceiling on Tier 4 bulk months; ops must enforce the contents
  envelope. At 3+ cats we already split shipments, so the mechanism exists.

### 2.8 Hygiene "clean pouch" → pharmacy wrap

Rev A grouped Tier 2 hygiene in a kraft drawstring pouch (~0.60, imported). Rev B:
a **flat kraft bag, pharmacy fold, closed with one sticker**, printed 1C in the same
local gang runs (0.25). Bands for treats are cut from the card press run's offcuts
(gang printing's free real estate) at ~0.10.

- **Why cheaper:** flat bags are a local commodity; gang-run printing; no import.
  Adder bundle 1.25 → 0.85 (T3), 0.35 (T2).
- **Why still premium:** the pharmacy fold + sticker is an Aesop gesture — quieter and
  more "care" than a drawstring pouch, which reads gift-shop.
- **Saving:** −0.40 (T3) / −0.25 (T2) per box.
- **Trade-offs:** the pouch was re-usable; the wrap is not. The annual drawer and den
  box carry the reuse story instead.

### 2.9 What is explicitly NOT cut

These survived every cost pass because they carry disproportionate perceived value:

| Kept | Cost | Why untouchable |
|---|---|---|
| 1C deep-green printed shipper | in shipper price | The colour equity *is* the brand's doorstep presence. Plain kraft + label would halve print cost and destroy the moat |
| Double-wall board under litter | in shipper price | A crushed box is the most expensive box ever shipped. Damage > 2% erases every saving in this document |
| The punch-out den door | ~0.15 | Highest perceived-value-per-riyal feature in the system; the sustainability story; the social loop |
| Seal sticker, monthly rotation | 0.12 | The collectible cadence signal; cheapest delight in the catalogue |
| The cat's name on the card | ~0.02 | R001. Non-negotiable |
| Cotton tape on Tier 4 | 0.35 | The single tactile "gift" cue on the top tier; paper substitutes read cheap |
| ISTA validation + cat-safety spec | process cost | Trust precedes everything (authority, emotions #2) |

## 4 · Jeddah-first supplier plan

Monthly flow becomes **100% Kingdom-local, Jeddah-anchored**. International sourcing
is eliminated from the monthly loop entirely; it remains only as a *price-check
benchmark* at annual re-tender (the compelling-reason test: if a local litho-lam
drawer quote exceeds ~6.50 SAR, Kumar Printers' ~8–12 SAR landed doesn't beat it
anyway once freight, inspection and buffer stock are counted — so local wins on
total cost of ownership, not sentiment).

| Role | Primary (Jeddah) | Backup | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shipper + Curtain blank (flexo, die-cut) | **UCIC** — plants in Jeddah; 265k-t capacity | Napco National (Jeddah/Dammam) | Negotiate as one kit: box + insert nested on shared board runs; ask for trim-waste credit on the nested die |
| Offset gang runs — card shells, bands, wraps, folio blanks | **Arabian Print Pack** (Jeddah, offset packaging specialist) | Exprint (Jeddah); Al-Madina Printing (Jeddah, up-to-10-colour offset) | Quarterly gang sheets: card + bands + wrap on shared plates = 30–40% off smalls |
| Foil + deboss (folio number, anniversary drawer) | **MMC Print n Gift** (Jeddah — hot foil, deboss, HP Indigo, kitting) | Riyadh: Printhub for short runs | Foil is one pass on one small item — keep it where the craft is |
| Stickers / seal labels | **Liva Print** (Jeddah — labels, eco packaging) | Zeej Print (Jeddah/Riyadh) | Monthly motif rotation = ink change only |
| Tissue, kraft bags, WAT tape stock | **Paperpros** (Jeddah paper consumables) + Napco paper division | local trading houses | All shelf items — zero custom MOQ |
| Litho-lam annual drawer | **UCIC litho-lam line** + MMC finishing | Obeikan (Riyadh) at scale; Silver Corner for gifting-grade | Annual volume is small; local short-run wins |
| Pilot/proto (all pieces) | MMC + Paperpros hand-work | **Printhub (Riyadh)** digital short-run | Riyadh used only where Jeddah lacks the digital short-run niche |

**Why Jeddah-first is itself a cost strategy:** the fulfilment centre's inbound freight
on corrugate (bulky, cheap per kg) is the hidden line item — a Jeddah mill-to-warehouse
loop runs days and pennies; imports run weeks and demand safety stock (working capital)
plus AQL inspection fees. Local also means press checks in person: one caught
mis-registration pays for the taxi for a decade.

## 5 · Zero-cost levers (do these regardless)

1. **Sequence is free.** The §05 choreography of Rev A survives intact — it was never
   the expensive part. Guard it in V1 film review.
2. **Words are free.** Every phrase — lid line, tape line, den door caption, card copy —
   is plate ink. Invest writer hours, not material.
3. **Precision is a die-maker conversation.** Demand ±0.5 mm on the comb wells and the
   curtain notch. Costs one meeting; reads as Apple forever.
4. **The app is the fireworks.** The QR box-moment (ceremony animation, savings tally)
   is already built product — every riyal of screen delight subsidises a riyal of
   board. Physical stays calm; digital celebrates (R073's richest-animation rule).
5. **The cat is the marketing department.** The den door turns every empty box into
   user-generated content. Zero media budget behaves like a campaign.

## 7 · Authority check (Rev B specifics)

- **Premium through restraint** — Rev B *increases* restraint: fewer pieces, one ink,
  scarcer foil. This moves the system closer to the authority's "subtraction
  discipline", not further.
- **R006 honesty** — cutting material the member never valued is honest; simulating
  luxury we don't ship would not be. Nothing here fakes: the folio is genuinely heavy
  stock, the foil is genuinely foil, the drawer is genuinely annual.
- **R031 ceremony** — preserved and concentrated: first box and anniversary are the two
  over-invested moments (matching the authority's "two moments" doctrine more tightly
  than Rev A's quarterly cadence did).
- **R041/R042 value visibility** — improved: the anniversary drawer prints the year's
  savings total inside the lid.
- **Emotions check** — belonging (unchanged), trust (unchanged + local provenance),
  pride (concentrated), ease (fewer pieces to unpack), delight (rationed, per doctrine),
  smart value (a leaner system the member would *approve of* if shown the math — the
  savvy-owner emotion applied to ourselves).
