MRC-PKG-001 · Physical Experience Strategy · 2026-07

The box is the membership card you can hold.

A complete packaging system for Moracat — from courier truck to the cat sleeping in the empty box.

Structural engineering Unboxing choreography Supplier matrix · KSA / GCC / CN / TR Unit economics · SAR Arabic-first · RTL

Moracat's revenue engine is a monthly care plan; its moat is a membership identity. The shipment is the only moment the membership becomes physical — the one time a month Moracat is standing in the member's hallway. This dossier designs that moment as a system: a shipping structure honest enough to carry twelve kilograms of litter and food across the Kingdom, a presentation layer worthy of the Cat ID ceremony, and an economic model that survives the 9 SAR average packaging budget the business already runs on (packages/db/prisma/box-cost-model.ts). Everything here obeys one reframe: the cat is the hero — and the cat, famously, loves the box.

00

Read this if you read nothing else

Executive summary

The verdict in one paragraph. Moracat should not ship a luxury rigid box every month — it should ship a beautifully engineered kraft system every month and reserve rigid luxury for the two moments that deserve it: the first shipment (the Cat ID ceremony arrives in a keepsake Welcome Folio) and Tier 4's quarterly keepsake (the Den Box). A 12 kg monthly box containing a 10 L litter bag physically cannot be a magnetic-closure rigid box, and pretending otherwise would cost 25–40 SAR per shipment and read as waste, not luxury. Apple's own lesson applies: premium is precision, pacing and restraint — not expensive board.

  • Architecture — "Stage over Cellar": one double-wall kraft shipper printed in one colour (the brand's deep green), internally split into a heavy cellar (litter, dry food, cans) and a light stage tray on top carrying tissue, the monthly card, and the discovery items. The lid opens onto ceremony; the weight hides below. Packaging cost 7.6–17 SAR by tier at 5k/mo — inside the current model's envelope.
  • The box's second life: every shipper carries a die-cut punch-out door and a printed interior so the empty box converts into a cat den. Sustainability, social sharing and the "cat is the hero" principle in one move — no extra material, ~0.15 SAR of die-cutting.
  • Four tiers, one system: the same shipper family in three sizes serves Essentials through Full Membership; tiers differ by contents, insert count and finishing accents, not by structure. Kitten boxes use a lighter single-wall mailer. Multi-cat ships per-cat kraft bands, each printed with the cat's name.
  • Suppliers: prototype locally (Printhub Riyadh / MMC Jeddah), scale corrugate with UCIC (Jeddah — 265k-tonne capacity), source the two rigid keepsake pieces factory-direct from China or Turkey (MOQ 500–1,000). Full matrix in §10.
  • Economics: at 5,000 boxes/month the blended packaging cost is ≈ 9.8 SAR — within ±1 SAR of the modelled 9 SAR — while the unboxing experience moves from "e-commerce carton" to benchmark. Verified against computeBoxEconomics invariants in §11.

The one-sentence brief

Design the shipment so the member feels "my cat's care has arrived" — never "my order has shipped" — and so the cat ends the day asleep inside the packaging.

Rev B supersedes the cost sections

The tray structure (§03), finish costs (§06), sourcing mix (§10) and cost tables (§11) are superseded by MRC-PKG-002 — Lean Luxury Revision (design/moracat-packaging-lean-luxury.md): a Jeddah-first rework that merges the stage tray into the divider, localises the Welcome Folio and keepsakes, and cuts blended packaging ≈ 22% (T4 −45%) with no reduction in the member-facing experience. The choreography, safety spec and design language in this document remain in force.

Synthesis — the five laws the benchmarks teach

1. Luxury is pacing, not board grade. 2. Own one colour and never dilute it. 3. Design the keepable component deliberately; let everything else be honestly disposable. 4. Heavy goods demand structural honesty — dignity comes from engineering, not decoration. 5. The best subscription packaging creates a ritual the member would miss.

Phase 2 · Understanding the business

The Moracat frame

Packaging decisions inherit the constitution: belonging is the product, savings are proof, the cat is the hero. And they inherit the ledger: a cost model already committed to code.

What the design authority dictates physically

  • R001 Recognition first → the member's cat is named on the monthly card, never "Dear customer."
  • R009 The cat is the hero → the box is designed for the cat's use after unboxing; photography features cats in boxes, not boxes alone.
  • Amendment 2026-07-10 → the box is never sold as a box: it is "your cat's care, handled." Copy on pack must say care, not commerce.
  • R031 Ceremony on issue → the first shipment carries the Cat ID reveal and is over-invested (the Welcome Folio, §03).
  • R006 Honest by default → no fake wax seals, no "limited edition" theatre, provenance printed plainly.
  • R101–R110 Arabic-first → Arabic reads first on every surface; the box opens correctly for an RTL reader (§08).
  • Premium is subtraction → one colour, one seal, one message per surface. No gold (Brand personality).

The subscription hierarchy this system serves

The packaging is designed against the four-tier care hierarchy (with the three live plans — Starter 249 / Standard 349 / Premium 529 SAR — mapping onto it during migration). Per the 2026-07-10 amendment, members never pick from a tier table; the plan is computed from the cat's profile. The hard physical facts

  • A 10 L litter bag weighs ≈ 8 kg and dominates every structural decision. The Standard box is ~12 kg gross (noted in box-cost-model.ts).
  • The committed cost model: packaging 9 SAR · delivery 12 SAR · fulfilment 6 SAR per box, PSP 5%, shrink 2%. Delivery is flagged as the most sensitive input and needs a real 3PL quote for ~12 kg.
  • Riyadh summer couriers reach 45 °C+: no chocolate-adjacent coatings, no low-melt adhesives, wet food must sit away from sun-facing panels; vans are assumed unrefrigerated.
  • Prices are VAT-inclusive SAR; invoices break out 15% — the packaging cannot promise what the P&L can't keep.

3.3 The ceremony pieces

The Welcome Folio (first box, every tier). A 2 mm greyboard folio, 230 × 160 × 18 mm, wrapped in uncoated deep-green paper, opening like a passport wallet: left leaf holds the printed Cat ID card in a die-cut cradle and the collar tag on a cotton loop; right leaf prints the cat's name, the human-readable ID number in brass foil, and a QR that adds the wallet pass. This is Stage 4's physical body (R031 R032 R034) — the single object a member keeps for years, and the only place monthly economics permit foil. Cost ≈ 11–14 SAR, paid once per member, booked as acquisition cost, not monthly packaging.

The Den Box (Tier 4, quarterly). A rigid drawer box, 280 × 200 × 90 mm, 1.5 mm board, kraft-lined drawer, cotton pull-tab, blind-debossed seal. Carries the seasonal gift, partner gifts and exclusives. Slide-open is the gesture cats and members both investigate. Amortised ≈ 4.5 SAR/month within Tier 4's packaging budget.

Phase 3 · continued

Inserts & protection

The spec is binary: nothing moves, and nothing that touches the box can hurt a cat.

The insert kit, piece by piece

  • Cellar divider set — one die-cut B-flute piece, self-locking (no glue, no staples — staples are a claw hazard), forming three cells: litter cradle (bag on edge, arch support so the bag cannot slump), dry-food cell (gusset up, brand face out), wet-food cells (cans in a 3×5 honeycomb; pouches in a fence-slot comb; 30 g E-flute pad between can layers). Assembly < 10 s trained.
  • Stage tray — E-flute, four-corner self-lock, sits on the divider's shoulder 60 mm below the lid so lid flaps never crush contents. Interior printed 1C green pattern. Two 15 mm cotton twill loops for lifting out.
  • Stage pad (Tier 1) — a single scored E-flute pad replaces the tray: folds over the cellar carrying card + tissue. Ceremony kept, cost halved.
  • Moulded pulp cradle — reserved for the Welcome Folio (holds folio + tag with Apple-grade fit) and for glass/ceramic partner gifts in the Den Box. Tooling ~USD 1–3k once; unit ~USD 0.10–0.35 at 3k+ (§10 sourcing).
  • Void control — crinkle kraft shred only (never plastic air pillows, never foam peanuts — instant ingestion hazard and off-brand). Target: zero void by design; shred is the tolerance eater, ≤ 15 g/box.

Cat-safety spec (non-negotiable)

  • Water/soy-based inks throughout; no mineral-oil offset inks on food-adjacent surfaces (mineral-oil migration is a food-contact risk).
  • No loose magnets, staples, curling ribbon, elastic loops, or glitter — all documented feline ingestion hazards. Cotton twill tape ≥ 15 mm wide only.
  • No applied fragrance anywhere; no essential oils (many are feline-toxic).
  • All die-cut edges on member-facing pieces are folded or hemmed; the punch-out door perforation is designed to leave a clean rounded rim.
  • Adhesives: starch-based or hot-melt rated food-adjacent; tissue is unbuffered, dye-fast (cats will sit on it — that's the point).

Transit validation

Every structural revision passes: ISTA-3A drop/vibration with full Tier 2 load; 40 °C/90% RH 24 h conditioning then repeat drops; litter-bag survival (no burst, no dust leak) after 10-drop abuse; can-dent count ≤ 1 minor dent per 15 cans. Field check: photo-audit the first 100 live deliveries per city (Riyadh, Jeddah, Dammam differ in handling roughness and humidity).

Phase 3 · the choreography

The opening sequence

Written as a test script — every beat has an owner, a feeling, and a failure mode. Total runtime: about 90 seconds of designed experience, then a lifetime of the cat using the box.

  1. 0:00 — The door

    Arrival

    The courier hands over a clean kraft box, deep-green seal visible on top. No plastic overwrap, no third-party branding except the shipping label in its printed frame (a label placed crooked on a designed frame still looks intentional). The Arabic wordmark reads first; the weight is honest in the hands via the die-cut handles. Feeling: "this is for us," before a single word is read.

  2. 0:10 — First touch

    Texture

    Unbleached kraft, visible flute at the edges, matte green ink you can feel sink into the fibre. Nothing glossy. The top panel prints one line beside the seal: لِحياة قطّك كلّها · "For your cat's whole life." Failure mode guarded: scuffed print — kraft + matte ink hides courier abuse that would ruin a white gloss box.

  3. 0:20 — The seal

    Opening the tape

    Reinforced water-activated kraft tape, printed with the brand's thin "thread" line and a small printed phrase at the cut point: افتح بهدوء — أحدهم يراقب · "Open gently — someone is watching." One knife pass along a printed guide line that keeps the blade away from contents and keeps the punch-out door intact. The first smile happens here, and it cost 0.4 SAR.

  4. 0:30 — The lid

    The reveal

    Flaps open to the lid interior printed edge-to-edge in green with one cream line: أهلاً بعودتك · "Welcome home." Below: the stage tray, dressed in cream tissue sealed with one sticker from the illustration set. The litter is invisible. Feeling: hotel turndown, not warehouse pick.

  5. 0:40 — The card

    Recognition

    On top of the tissue: the monthly card, addressed to the cat by name — لِلوز، تموين شهر أغسطس · "For Louz — August's provisions." Inside: this month's contents list (Arabic first), the member-rate saving for this box stated plainly (R041 R085), one seasonal care note written like a good vet's advice, and the QR. First box only: the Welcome Folio sits here instead, in its pulp cradle — the Cat ID ceremony in the hand.

  6. 0:55 — The tissue

    Discovery

    Sticker lifted, tissue parts to the tier's discovery items — treats fold-wrapped in kraft bands (Tier 3+), the hygiene pouch (Tier 2+), the toy or seasonal gift (Tier 4). Each band prints what the item is and why it was chosen for this cat. Nothing inside the stage requires scissors.

  7. 1:10 — The cellar

    The provisions

    Cotton loops lift the stage tray out; below, the month's staples stand in their cells like a well-packed pantry: litter cradled, dry food face-up, cans in their comb. No shred storm, no chaos. Feeling: competence. "They handled it." (Ease/relief — emotion #4.)

  8. 1:25 — The scan

    The bridge to the app

    The card's QR opens the app's box moment: delivery confirmed, this month's saving added to the running tally (R042), pantry updated, and one line of guidance ("Louz's next weigh-in is due"). Physical and digital membership close the loop in one gesture.

  9. 1:40 — The second life

    The cat takes over

    The card's last panel shows the punch-out door: press, fold, and the empty shipper becomes بيت اللوز — "Louz's den." The tissue goes in as bedding; the cat moves in; the member photographs it; the brand's most honest advertising posts itself. The box is never garbage on day one.

Design guardrails for the sequence

One clear action per beat (R-principle 5); no beat depends on reading English; a member who rips everything open in 10 seconds loses nothing functional — pacing is offered, never enforced (calm over clever, R-principle 7).

Phase 6 · Visual system

Design language

The digital system already exists — "warm paper, flat stickers, editorial confidence" (packages/ui/src/styles/globals.css). The physical system is the same voice in fibre and ink: Apple's restraint, Hermès' colour conviction, Muji's honesty — with a Najdi warmth none of them have.

Palette · exterior

Kraft + one green

Unbleached kraft ground; deep green #045b46 as the only exterior ink. The green box becomes the brand's silhouette on every doorstep — colour equity built monthly.

Palette · interior

The warm reveal

Cream tissue (#f7dec9 family), warm-paper card stock, sticker accents — tangerine #f86c2f, blush, sage, butter — used like enamel pins: one or two per box, never a confetti of them.

Typography

Arabic leads

Premium Arabic display (Lyon Arabic Display class) set first and largest (R101 R103); Fraunces for English display; IBM Plex Sans for utility; the Cat ID number in IBM Plex Mono — the "engineering truth" voice.

Illustration

Flat stickers, rationed

The existing flat-sticker set (cat, sprig, heart, paw in blue-grey/butter/peach) appears as the seal sticker, the tissue motif, and the monthly sticker sheet — and nowhere else. The paw is a rare guest, never wallpaper.

Layout

Editorial confidence

One message per surface. Generous margins. The lid interior is a title page, the card is an article, the bands are captions. Minimalism where structure speaks; expression only in the sticker moments.

RTL-native

Built right-to-left

Panels compose for an RTL reading arc: Arabic top-right anchor, English lower-left echo (R104). The card opens right-to-left. English is the translation, visibly.

The three influences, held in tension

  • Saudi luxury — not ornament but hospitality: the box as a host. Generosity of space, the majlis instinct of presenting the best first, dates-box gifting culture informing the Den Box. Sadu-derived geometry may appear as a single thin woven line on the tape — never as pastiche.
  • Modern Japanese — the fold as respect: kraft bands with one fold instead of adhesive; the stage/cellar concept itself is a bento logic; "enough" as a design position.
  • Premium Scandinavian — functional warmth: self-locking structures shown honestly, flute edges visible, instructions that read like furniture assembly done right.

What is banned

Paw-print patterns, fish-bone icons, cartoon whiskers, "meow" puns in display type, gold foil floods, glossy lamination, mascot eyes on the box. The register is a fine grocer who knows your cat's name — never a toy store (Brand personality).

Phase 7 · Responsibility as value

Sustainability

The system is designed so the greenest choice and the most premium choice are the same choice — and the cat is the recycling program.

  • Mono-material by design: shipper, trays, dividers, bands, cards — all paper fibre, all one recycling stream. FSC-certified liners and stocks throughout; water/soy-based inks; starch adhesives; water-activated paper tape. No plastic film, foam, bubble, or magnet anywhere in the monthly flow.
  • The punch-out den is the sustainability program: a shipper that becomes furniture isn't waste diverted — it's waste that never happened. Reuse-by-design beats recycle-by-instruction, and it is the single most on-brand move available (R009).
  • Forever pieces, refill logic (AG1 lesson): treat tin, scoop, cotton pouch arrive once; refills arrive flat/light thereafter. Waste per month falls while shelf presence — perceived value — becomes permanent.
  • Right-sizing: three shipper sizes + computed plans mean air is never shipped; void shred ≤ 15 g. Double-wall board is itself ≥ 70% recycled content (standard for KSA corrugate mills).
  • Local production = shipped-emissions honesty: corrugate from Jeddah/Riyadh mills (§10) cuts inbound freight to near zero versus importing finished boxes; only the two rigid ceremony pieces travel far, and they travel flat-ish and rarely.
  • Say it plainly, once: one line on the base panel — "This box is 100% paper. The door on the side is for {the cat}." No green badges, no eco-theatre (R006).

What the volume curve buys (savings vs 100-unit pilot cost)

T1: −33% at 500 → −50% at 1k → −63% at 5k → −68% at 10k. The curve's knee is at ~5,000/mo — the point where UCIC tooling amortises and the blended average across a realistic tier mix (40% T1 · 30% T2 · 20% T3 · 10% T4, kitten ≈ 8% of base) lands at ≈ 9.8 SAR/box: within one riyal of the committed model. Below 1k/mo, packaging runs 3–7 SAR hot — an accepted, temporary pilot cost, not a pricing input.

Adjacent costs the packaging decision moves

  • Assembly: all-flat-pack keeps pack-out at ~3.5 min/box (within the 6 SAR fulfilment line at KSA labour rates); a rigid monthly box would have added ~1.5 min + assembled-storage handling.
  • Storage: flat corrugate for 10k boxes ≈ 2 pallet positions/day of production; assembled rigid would need ~6×. Ceremony pieces (small, dense) add one position per quarter.
  • Shipping: dimensional weight is unchanged by this design (contents dominate); the 12 SAR delivery line item stays the sensitive one flagged in box-cost-model.ts — negotiate against the ~12 kg reality, and note the storefront's 25 SAR customer-facing shipping fee needs reconciliation.
  • Damage/shrink: ISTA-validated structure should hold transit damage under the modelled 2% shrink; every 0.5% saved ≈ 0.6–1.2 SAR/box at tier COGS — the divider set pays for itself in dented cans alone.

Invariant check — the go/no-go gate

Before launch, each tier recipe runs through computeBoxEconomics with the V1-quoted packaging figures substituted for packagingSar (per-tier env overrides: BOX_PACKAGING_SAR). Gate: ≥15% member saving, ≥25% contribution, post-VAT survival. The Welcome Folio is excluded from the per-box model and booked to acquisition; the Den Box is inside Tier 4's recipe cost. No tier ships until its row is green.

Phase 10 · Iteration plan

Prototype roadmap

  1. Weeks 1–4 · ~100 units · Printhub + MMC

    Prototype V1 — "Prove the choreography"

    Build: digital-printed M-2 + hand-cut divider + stage tray; MMC-made Welcome Folio (foil, deboss); full dressing kit. Test: ISTA-3A drops with real litter; 10 member households filmed unboxing (Arabic-first participants); pack-out timing; 45 °C van simulation. Learn: does the stage/cellar reveal land emotionally? Does the punch-out door survive transit intact? Is the divider one-piece fold actually <10 s?

  2. Weeks 5–12 · ~1,000 units · UCIC tooling + near-shore rigid

    Prototype V2 — "Prove the economics"

    Change & why: flexo plates + die tooling at UCIC (unit cost −45%, print consistency up); divider revised from V1 drop data (targeted material trim −10% board); folio moved to Kumar/KBC sample lots for landed-cost comparison; tissue coverage reduced if V1 film review shows it reads busy (restraint check); punch-out door perforation gauge tuned to open clean but never in transit. Test: live cohort shipping in Riyadh + Jeddah; damage telemetry vs 2% shrink line; first NPS-of-unboxing measure; recycling/reuse survey ("where is the box now?").

  3. Months 4–6 · 5,000+/mo capability · dual-sourced

    Prototype V3 — "Prove the machine"

    Change & why: pre-print interior liner replaces post-print at volume (lid interior cost −30%, registration tighter); Napco onboarded as shipper second source (resilience); moulded-pulp folio cradle tooled in China alongside Den Box production (container-sharing); M-3 and K-mailer dies cut; per-cat band VDP automated into the pick line. Test: full ISTA re-validation on every SKU; cost audit vs §11 tables; Tier 4 quarterly Den cadence dry-run; 10k/mo line-rate simulation at 3PL. Exit criteria: blended packaging ≤ 10 SAR, damage < 1%, unboxing NPS ≥ 70, every tier green on invariants.

Each iteration improves all six mandated axes — durability (drop data → structure), luxury (film review → pacing edits), unboxing (member observation → beat timing), sustainability (material trims, reuse telemetry), cost (tooling amortisation), manufacturing efficiency (line-rate tests) — and the reason for every change is written down before the change is made.

The decision

Final recommendation

Adopt the Green Box system: the kraft "Stage over Cellar" M-Shipper family (UCIC-manufactured, deep-green one-colour, punch-out cat door), dressed with tissue, seal sticker, printed WAT tape and a name-personalised monthly card; the rigid Welcome Folio once per member carrying the Cat ID ceremony; the rigid Den Box quarterly for Tier 4; the K-Mailer for kittens; per-cat name bands for multi-cat homes.

It is the only candidate that wins on all five axes simultaneously:

  • Luxury — delivered through pacing, paper, print and recognition (the choreography of §05), with true rigid luxury reserved for the moments that carry identity, exactly as the authority's ceremony rules demand.
  • Customer delight — the lid message, the cat's name, the monthly sticker, and a box the cat keeps. The delight compounds monthly instead of peaking once.
  • Operational efficiency — 100% flat-pack monthly flow, one structure across tiers, in-Kingdom replenishment measured in days, 3.5-minute pack-out.
  • Scalability — UCIC/Napco carry 10,000+/month without strain; ceremony pieces batch quarterly from proven exporters with second sources named.
  • Profitability — blended ≈ 9.8 SAR at 5k/mo against the modelled 9 SAR, with every tier gated by checkBoxInvariants before launch, and the folio booked honestly to acquisition.

This is how Moracat becomes the unboxing benchmark for the Kingdom and the GCC: not by shipping the most expensive box in the category, but by shipping the most considered one — the box that knows the cat's name on the way in, and belongs to the cat afterwards. The carton is temporary; the membership it performs is not.
